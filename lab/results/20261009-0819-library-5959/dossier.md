# Dossier — library lens, run 20261009-0819-library-5959

**Claim.** The slack skin must be a per-body binary law sized by measured drift — `s_i = sqrt(R·d_i/c_keep)`, and *no skin at all* for bodies drifting faster than ~R per rebuild (the concentrated minority) — because one global width loses 1.8–4× in maintenance work and 2–4.5× in worst-body work on mixed belts; plus four more decision-changing lessons from Julien's library for the in-flight builds.

**Bottleneck.** The board's top scaling risk (candidate work must track real encounters; the slack skin is the adopted lever, 12.6×). Gain: deletes global-constant tuning and the re-pay storm for concentrated minorities; order-of-magnitude proof below on work-rate structure, not the engine.

- Global s gives a clean U (thin side ×3.3, fat side ×4.3 on quiet); argmin matches `sqrt(R·d_med/c_keep)` within 1.5× on quiet, ×2.5 off on kicked (heterogeneous drift; grid min is a plateau s∈[4,8]).
- The naive per-body sqrt law **loses** to the best global skin in the formation case: wide skins for the hot 5% overbuild the window (keep cost ∝ s) — skins track `sqrt(drift)`, but drift fast enough (≥ ~R per rebuild lifetime) makes any skin lose, because the rebuild rate caps at one per orbit.
- The winning rule is binary and local: `d_i ≥ R → no skin` (re-pay per orbit; 3.8% of bodies in formation — hand exactly these to the phase-neighbour certificate), else the sqrt law. Neither a p99-calibrated global width nor a blanket per-body law is right (p99-global: 4.1× more work, 4.3× worse p99 body than composite, on formation).

Failed: P2's tail clause (got 2.0×, predicted ≥3×); P3 almost entirely (p99-calibrated global is *not* within 1.3× — it is 1.9–4.1× off — though its worst-body work does match on quiet belts); P1's 1.5× clause on the kicked case. The failures sharpened the rule: the correct object is the binary composite, which I had not written down in advance. Disclosure: v1 of the probe had inconsistent units (R wider than the whole belt) and an unsaturated window; v2 (this file) fixed both and added the composite. v1 output is kept as `skin-probe-out-v1-disclosed.txt`.

**What would make it wrong.** (1) The c_keep/c_eval price on the M1: memory-vs-compute at a different ratio moves s* (∝ sqrt of the ratio's inverse) but not the U-shape or the binary rule. (2) Formation drift is correlated in reality (my toy drifts bodies independently); correlated drift can only widen the no-skin minority. (3) If a real belt's drift distribution has no separation near d = R, the binary rule degrades gracefully to the sqrt law (no cliff). (4) The toy assumes the drift bound is conservative (no missed candidates); the engine's own gates must verify that, as r8 v2 did. Cloud numbers are order-of-magnitude; the integrator re-measures on the M1.

**Cost of adoption.** Small: one per-body skin field plus a one-time drift-calibration pass (a measured belt property), replacing a hand-tuned global constant in the in-flight `b-encounter-skin` build.

**Fit with PRINCIPLES.** Principle 22: "the simulation tells us what's next not us, subtle but deadly" — the width is *measured* drift, not a chosen constant. Principle 33: "a body resolves only the influences that are significant at its own scale" — quiet bodies buy skins, the hot minority pays per orbit. Principle 3: compute only surprises — the skin exists to not re-derive unchanged conjunctions.

## The five lessons (deliverable)

1. **Skin width is a law, not a number — and it is binary per body.** Taleb, *Antifragile*: "Redundancy is ambiguous because it seems like a waste if nothing unusual happens. Except that something unusual happens—usually." Mechanism: the skin is redundancy against drift; work(s) is U-shaped with `s* = sqrt(R·d/c_keep)`, and drift ≥ R per rebuild caps the rebuild rate at 1/orbit, where any skin loses. Change (`b-encounter-skin`, `q-skin`): per-body rule — hot bodies get no skin (→ phase-neighbour certificate), quiet bodies get `sqrt(R·d_i/c_keep)` from measured drift quantiles. Test: done above (`skin_probe.py`); the build re-measures the U in its own counters on belt 0.
2. **B12 should judge the error's response to shock size, not its recurrence.** Taleb, *Antifragile*: "For the fragile, the cumulative effect of small shocks is smaller than the single effect of an equivalent single large shock." Mechanism: bounded-vs-compounding separates as the *slope* of the error-growth exponent against kick size (2^k): fragile ⇒ slope > 0, robust ⇒ 0. Change (`q-b12-design`): per-seed slope of log-error-growth-exponent vs log(kick size), replacing the recurrence ratio that redteam-b12-stat showed cannot separate a slow leak from a bounded error. Test: one chaotic binary, kicks at 4 sizes, tens of lines.
3. **Delete the window setup before you cache it.** Isaacson, *Elon Musk*: "Always wait until the end of designing a process—after you have questioned all the requirements and deleted unnecessary parts—before you introduce automation"; "Step one should be to question the requirements … And then delete, delete, delete." Mechanism: 92% of per-kick evals are window setup (components-4643); a skin over un-deleted setup pays interest on it at every rebuild. Change: `b-encounter-skin`'s first milestone is driving setup cost per rebuild to 0; the 12.6× lever is unreachable while setup dominates. Test: the build's setup-vs-fire counters, per components methodology.
4. **H_live counts distinctions the future consumes, not what gets written.** Forte, *Building a Second Brain*: "Every time you take a note, ask yourself, 'How can I make this as useful as possible for my future self?' … Your notes will be useless if you can't decipher them in the future, or if they're so long that you never even try." Mechanism: layered compression is sized by reads; bits never read are free to drop (principle 24's floor). Change (`b-h-live`): define distinctions-per-game-second by *read* counters (fields a later event consumes), not writes; scars store only consumed distinctions. Test: instrument the belt for one game-second, report reads/writes.
5. **Triggers are binary local rules, judged at the tail.** Taleb, *Skin in the Game*: "Outcomes are paradoxically more stable under the minority rule—the variance of the results is lower and the rule is more likely to emerge independently across separate populations. What emerges from the minority rule is more likely to be black-and-white, binary rules." Mechanism: graded global widths lose to a binary local rule by 1.8–4× work and 2–4.5× worst-body work (probe, formation case); binary rules emerge independently per region (principle 15). Change: the skin trigger (d ≥ R) and the co-orbital certificate's concentration trigger both fire as local dimensionless numbers; gates judge them at the adversarial concentration quantile (B18 belt precedent). Test: the formation case above; in the engine, sweep formation fraction as the negative control.
