Grade: silver

**Abstract.** The kick's byte bill and the read's cold-cache tax, measured for the first time on shipped code. Reproduced with counters bit-identical across four executions: 9.6–19.7 KB and 15.5–16.9 allocs per fired kick over N=10³–3×10⁴, a bandwidth floor of 0.18–1.2 µs/event, ≥43× under the kick's compute — the event path is compute-bound and at 10⁹ the wall is capacity, not bandwidth; the read's cold tax is 62–92% shared footprint, the knot table innocent (statics verified in source). My adversarial arms (e=0.9999, N=30000, a zero-kick attribution probe) all strengthen the acquittal. Silver: two of four pre-registered predictions died, the headline needed five numeric corrections, allocs/kick conflates background. Adoption cost: none.

**Reasons.**

*Reproduction.* Patch is print-only (`bwfloor` example; engine and flights untouched). I applied it to the clean tree and ran `regen.sh` end to end: sizes and both wave counters **bit-identical** (N=3000: 577,535 allocs / 650,057,850 B / 34,537 kicks; N=10000: 687,214 / 506,974,074 / 41,537) — the instrument is deterministic. Micro timings within the disclosed ±25% wobble in shape, with one real exception: the e=0.01 hot read is 2.0–2.2 µs in three executions (regen, mine, and the author's own regen.log), not the dossier's "2.9–3.3 µs at every e".

*Predictions.* P3 (15–35 KB/kick; measured 12.2–18.8) and P4 (roofline acquittal) held. P1 was killed at every key (55–158% vs the pre-registered ≥50% rule) and honestly reported; P2 killed at low e. But the dossier reports P2 as "held at e ≥ 0.6 (23%→5%)" while its own kill rule is ≥15% — e=0.6 measures 24–25% in both independent reruns, so P2 holds only at e ≥ 0.9 (12–13%). Misreporting a prediction against its own pre-registered rule is exactly what the lab's rules exist to catch.

*Adversarial (my own arms, all outside the author's tested set).*
1. **e=0.999 / e=0.9999** (177 / 230 knots, beyond the ladder's 124): read cold delta 3.57 / 2.75 µs (flat), data-only 0.65 / −0.12 µs (zero within noise at 230 knots), compile tax 5% / 4%; compile write bill 49,096 B (2.4–3.1× the 15.6–20.2 KB table). The "flat in knots" and acquittal claims hold a decade deeper in (1−e).
2. **N=30000** (beyond 10⁴): 15.5 allocs and 9,601 B per kick, deterministic. Bytes fall **below** the dossier's 12.2–18.8 KB range — "flat in N" is true for allocs, not bytes; the envelope over N=10³–3×10⁴ is 9.6–19.7 KB. Direction favorable: 9.6 KB → 565 ns at 17 GB/s, so the acquittal only strengthens.
3. **Attribution probe** (`wave 3000 2`: 1 frame, 0 kicks → 190 allocs, 18,150 B/frame background): non-kick allocation is ≤3.4% of the byte bill (bytes verdict safe) but, if steady, up to ~40% of the alloc count — the 16.5–16.7 allocs/kick is total÷kicks and overstates kick-owned allocs; the marginal mid-flight rate is ≈14–15 allocs/kick (frames 3→7: +1,702 allocs / +111 kicks). The dossier never bounds this.
4. **Statics verified in source**: `crates/space/src/tables.rs` — `EDGE`/`ATAN` are `[i128; 17]` (272 B each), `X`/`W` `[i128; 2]`; the shared-footprint attribution's "sub-KB statics" is exact.
5. **Domain edge verified**: at p=7, `OrbitKey::bound` succeeds but `Orbit::compile` returns `NoBoundOrbit` for e ≥ 0.60 — the strong-field cold-tax claim cannot extend past e=0.30; the author's p=7 probe sat at the domain boundary without saying so.

*Novelty.* Bytes/event and the bandwidth floor were never measured (HISTORY's bytes-per-frame entries are view encoding; the wiki's only GB/s is calendar's rescan floor). Partial priors: **fermi-061f** (silver, 13 Oct) already counted 9 allocs/kick on another protocol — allocs-per-kick was not first here, and neither dossier nor page cites it; **4ea0** (gold, yesterday) had already concluded "sharing, not eviction" for the read side. What is genuinely new: the quantitative roofline (compute-bound by ≥43–900×) and the shared-vs-data cold-tax split with the code-warm split arm.

*Page.* The fold-in is faithful in structure (Current best + Refuted + Runs). Two overstatements remained after the first checker's in-place corrections — the headline byte range with "flat in N", and the Runs line's "2.2× the table" — both corrected in place by me (now 9.6–19.7 KB / allocs flat / 2.2–4×).

*Goal fit.* Answers PRINCIPLES 17 ("deeply aware of von-neumann bottleneck and memory") where it actually bites, and prices table-layout/compression as non-levers on the kick's wall (≤1.2 µs of 50–165 µs), keeping the deletion budget on the compute program. It deletes a candidate lever before anyone built it; it does not move a bottleneck by 10×. Zero adoption cost.

*Why silver, not gold.* Gold requires the held-out predictions to have held: two of four died on their own pre-registered kill rules, and the surviving mechanism (shared footprint) is a post-hoc diagnosis whose quantitative split wobbles 62–92% across three executions. The dossier's headline needed five numeric corrections against the evidence (hot range, shared share, churn 2.2×→2.2–4×, byte envelope/flat-in-N, "≤1% of the shipped kick" holding only at the 68 GB/s roof — 2.2% at the box's 17 GB/s). The load-bearing verdict itself survived everything I threw at it.

**The one experiment to gold.** Re-register predictions on the corrected mechanism and ranges (hot 1.9–3.1 µs at planet keys; shared 62–92%; data-only 0.5–1.6 µs; envelope 9.6–19.7 KB and 15.5–16.9 allocs/kick over N=10³–3×10⁴; background-corrected allocs/kick), then run one fresh held-out set — a new seed, mid-ladder e=0.75, one stronger p within the compile domain, and the strong-e plane population the author admits was never flown. All hold ⇒ gold.
