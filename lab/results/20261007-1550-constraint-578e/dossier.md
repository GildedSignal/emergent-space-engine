# Dossier — 20261007-1550-constraint-578e (lens: the constraint)

**Claim.** On main today the plane's presented frame is constrained by the **view gather, not the engine**: an unbudgeted O(all bodies) walk that takes 68% of frame CPU at the player's operating point (rtf 100), whose unit is the ~25 µs compiled-cycle readout at the emitter's retarded tick paid 1–3× per body per frame — 2.4 ms/frame at 28 bodies, 5.3 ms at 244 — while the engine sits under its 5 ms budget at 20%; the view has no budget at all, breaking plane.md §7's own rule ("nothing takes an unbounded synchronous claim on the frame").

**Bottleneck.** Board focus #1 (kicks) works the engine's 20%; the whole-frame measurement says the presented path's constraint is the gather. Expected gain from the cheapest exploit: 2–3× on the gather (1.6 ms/frame at rtf 100) by getting the crossing guess to its own contract; representation-level (budgeted/culled gather): removes the frame's only unbounded claim and is *required* before body counts grow ~10× (the gather, not the engine, hits the 16.7 ms deadline first at ~200–800 visible bodies).

**Evidence** (Linux x86, 8 vCPU, load 0.5–1.8, orders of magnitude only; all commands ≤ 60 s, binary built once with `cargo build --release -p xtask`):

- Whole frame, paced 20 s: `xtask universe --bench --rtf 1 --seconds 20` → frame_cpu p50 3.29 ms: view_gather 72.0%, view_json 16.1%, wire 7.0%, **engine 4.9%**. `--rtf 100 --seconds 20` → p50 4.52 ms: **view_gather 67.9%** (p50 3.57), engine 19.5% (p50 0.26, p99 5.21 = its 5 ms budget), 43/1200 overflow frames, rtf median 100.0.
- Gather internals (instrumentation in this run's patch, `VIEWPHASES` on stderr): at rtf 100, bodies loop 2,373 µs/frame of the 3,570 (66%), setup 69 µs, fleet loop 48 µs. 27 gathered bodies → 88 µs/body/frame at **3.1 readouts/body** (85 readouts). At rtf 1: 937 µs/frame, 35 µs/body, **1.6 readouts/body**. Per-readout cost stable at **~22–28 µs** across both regimes — that readout (`binding_at` + `credit` ramp + compiled-cycle `decode` per doll level, observe.rs `state_rate`) is the unit of the constraint.
- The 1-readout contract is achievable at rtf 100: `--rtf 100 --seconds 10 --members 400` → 381 members handed out, 165 bodies lost, ~244 live at end; late-run bodies loop 5,237 µs/frame at **1.07 readouts/body** (261 readouts), 21 µs/body. Gather p99 5.63 ms uncapped; engine p99 6.30 ms capped by budget, 139/600 overflow frames. Raw JSON: `bench-members400-rtf100.json`.
- Engine internals for contrast: `xtask universe --kick-bench --seconds 20` → kick rebind median 62 µs, leavers 366 µs, **memo hit rate 0.0%** (exact and quantized; the recompile-per-kick state confirmed on main). `engine_cost_by_kind` at rtf 100: player 1.29–1.86 ms/item (the most expensive item in the engine), ships 63–270 µs, fleet 47–49 µs.
- Gates: `xtask behave --law plane` → G1–G6/B7/B9–B11 fail *vacuously* (the plane's Law adapter answers `None` to every cycle question, plane.rs:80 — do not read these as regressions); the speaking gates match the handoff exactly (B13 60/64, B12 miscalibrated as known, B14 60.0 s vs the 60 s limit under load 1.8). `xtask engine-judge` → E1–E5, E7, E8 pass; E9 fails phase 1 as designed; E6 needs `--prepare-mutants`.

**Predictions** (`predictions.md`, written before the runs): P2's direction held (gather grows linearly, is the larger uncapped term, view p99 5.63 ms) but its magnitude was ~2× pessimistic — handed-out bodies gather at 21 µs/frame (1.07 readouts), not 60–90; the founding 27 cost 88 (3.1 readouts). P1 was wrong in an informative way: at rtf 300 unpaced the engine still keeps up (3/300 overflow) — but the run is contaminated (1,500 s of game time depopulates the scene; 165 losses at members 400), so "where the constraint moves" is answered by the members run, not the rtf sweep: the engine's budget binds (23% overflow at 244 bodies) at roughly the same body count where the gather crosses 5 ms — both sit on the cliff, only one has a brake. P3 held with a refinement: readouts/body degrade with the per-frame tick advance (1.6 at rtf 1 → 3.1 at rtf 100) — the guess memo's lever arm, not its existence, is the defect; per-readout cost is rtf-independent.

**What would make it wrong.** If the M1's gather/json shares differ materially (x86 float paths here), or if the flight view's real browser path amortises the gather differently than the bench loop (it does not gather for a browser here). If handed-out members at longer horizons regress to 3 readouts (they are early leavers on fresh orbits), the 21 µs/body figure is optimistic. The 88 µs founding-body figure embeds this scene's doll mix (orbiters in doll 2 near the player); a different scene could sit closer to 21.

**Cost of adopting.** The exploit (tighten the crossing guess to its 1-readout contract: second-order arrival-rate predictor or a bracket-seeded Newton step) is a local change to `ViewCache::put/guess`, touches no physics (the guess never decides the result, view.rs:104), no gates. The elevation (give the gather the engine's budget/slack treatment — cull by what the player differentiates, "the zoom level is the minimap", OC#30) is a design decision for the integrator: it changes what the debug view guarantees per frame, and must not change observables' bits.

**Fit with PRINCIPLES.** "Compute only surprises" (#3): between frames an emitter's crossing advances at the arrival rate; recomputing every body's full state every frame at 25 µs is the opposite of deriving. "No work for no reason" (#14) and "bounded work per presented frame" (plane.md §7): the gather is the one unbounded claim left. "The right question, then the representation" (#18): the gather's question is "what changed at the player's eye since last frame", asked today as "recompute everything".

**The lens may have made me miss.** Goldratt's lens ranks the *present* system; the goal's scaling constraint (kicks, encounters, candidate work at 10⁹) is real and the board's order is not wrong for it — this dossier reorders the *presented path only*. I did not measure the browser/HTTP path, GPU gather, or multi-thread behaviour (`--threads`), and the Linux numbers are orders of magnitude, as the README requires. The readout unit cost is shared by engine ramps, so the cycle memo Sonnet is wiring may move this constraint while aimed at kicks — worth re-measuring after it lands.
