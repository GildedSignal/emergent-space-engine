# Dossier — the compile's panel grid versus its information floor

**Claim.** The shipped compile walk's panel grid (the fixed EDGE ladder, 2^(-j/16) band
edges ≈ 16 panels per octave of x) runs **4.8–10.5× above the information floor** of the
law's own 2⁻³⁰ certificate on the whole expensive band (e ≥ 0.9, where the compile costs
76–500 µs), and **a floor-sized grid is already O(1)-in-e (12–27 panels at every p ≥ 20,
all e ≤ 0.99999)** — so the record's "panel count, a law change" lever needs no Carlson
kernel and no closed forms: an error-equidistributed **integer grid replacement cuts the
compile 4.8–10.5× on the same machinery**, and the Carlson-built crossing table
(closed-form-kernel's open direction 2) is **priced dead as a compile lever** (a
floor-sized table of 12–55 knots at de99's 6.5–30 µs per incomplete 3-channel evaluation
costs 78–1650 µs against the thinned walk's ~14–60 µs).

**Bottleneck.** The kick (scoreboard: 75–160 µs vs a ~42–55 ns floor, ~10³·³; the compile
is 49–97% of every kick rebind, and panel count is the record's one remaining ≥10× lever,
kick-jet open direction 1 / closed-form-kernel open direction 1). Expected gain: ~5–10×
on the compile term on the belt band and the leaver tail, deleting nothing else — the
walk, its integer quad, its readout and CAP machinery all stay. This measures the floor
the record never measured: every prior panel-number (5→176 knots, 3.2→197 µs) was read
off the shipped ladder, which is an a priori construction, not an error-driven one.

**Evidence** (`lab/results/20261014-0450-components-5a3b/panel_floor.csv`, regenerated by
`regen.sh` → `cargo run --release --example panel_floor -p emergent-space`, ~5 s; the
example reads shipped code only — `Orbit::compile`, `knots()`, the increments — and never
patches the walk). 45 keys: p ∈ {4.5, 6, 8, 20, 50, 1000, 10⁵} × e ∈ {0 … 0.99999} (the
p ≤ 6, e = 0 rows hit the symbolic-circle ISCO branch and are excluded as NaN).

- **The floor.** Per key, greedy bisection of [0, π] with the walk's own 4-point
  Gauss–Legendre rule per panel, against a GL16 reference per panel, summed error ≤ 2⁻³⁰
  relative on **all three channels** (φ, t, τ) — the compile's verified certificate
  (21ba: 3.0–4.8×10⁻¹⁰). N* = 12–27 panels for every p ≥ 20 at e ∈ [0.9, 0.99999],
  versus the ladder's 68–284. With the readout's CAP = π/4 constraint (the Newton solve)
  the practical floor N_cap = N* + ≤3 (12–30).
- **The idiot index** (shipped panels / N_cap): e ≤ 0.1: 1.25–2.0; e 0.5: 3.7–4.0;
  e 0.9: 5.2–5.8; e 0.99: 4.8–6.6; e 0.999: 4.7–7.7; e 0.9999: 5.3–8.9;
  e 0.99999: **5.7–10.5**. Flat-to-rising in e at every p (the ladder grows 16/octave;
  the floor grows only as the layer and the 1/x² spread, i.e. log-like).
- **The oracle is validated two ways (P6).** (i) The shipped increments match my float
  integrals to 1.6–6×10⁻¹⁰ relative on every p ≥ 20 key (advance = 2qI_φ, period =
  proper = 2I·(p·k)^{3/2} — the key stores periapsis·k, the wiki's `q_key = 1/(p·k)`
  note, which the first probe build got wrong and the dump caught: with q = 1/(p·k) the
  ladder's mystery knots reproduce exactly, e.g. √(δ/qa)/4 at (8, 0.1)). (ii) At p = 8
  the advance channel drifts to 5.9×10⁻⁸/3.7×10⁻⁷ at e 0.999/0.99999 — the record's own
  shipped-walk m→1 degradation (21ba check2: 6.7×10⁻⁷ at P = 3×10⁻⁹), which my per-panel
  GL16 reference does not share (t/τ agree at 10⁻¹¹ there). The ladder's own summed
  panel error sits at 10⁻¹²–10⁻¹³ for p ≥ 20 — the ladder is certifiably *safe*, just
  ~5–10× too dense.
- **Compile projection** (measured 1.1–1.7 µs/knot on this shared Linux/x86 box, orders
  of magnitude only): (50, 0.99999): 283 knots / 395 µs → 30 panels ≈ 35–50 µs (**8–11×**);
  (20, 0.99): 124/175 µs → 22 ≈ 26–38 µs (**4.6–6.7×**); the low-e band (e ≤ 0.1) has
  nothing to win (already 1.25–2× over floor), matching the Carlson pricing's own shape.
- **Carlson table priced dead (P5).** At de99's 6.5–30 µs per incomplete 3-channel
  evaluation, a floor-sized table (12–55 knots) costs 78–1650 µs — more than the shipped
  walk itself at most keys, and 4.6–24× the thinned walk at every key. The compile win
  the Carlson line promises (O(1) in e) is delivered equally by the thinned grid,
  because the floor itself is flat in e.

**Predictions** (`predictions.md`, written before the run): P1 (ratio ≥ 8 on the belt
band) **partial** — holds at e ≥ 0.9999 (8.8–10.5×), under-shoots at e 0.9–0.99
(4.8–6.6×); P2 (ratio rising in e, N* log-like) **held**; P3 (collapse to ~1–2× at
e ≤ 0.1) **held** (1.25–2.0); P4 (separatrix keys drop to ≤ 4×) **failed** — p = 8 reads
4.0–5.7×: the 16/octave ladder overshoots even the layer region, because a square-root
endpoint layer needs geometric doubling (≈1 panel per octave) plus CAP chunks, not 16
steps per octave; P5 **held** (arithmetic above); P6 (oracle sanity, the kill criterion)
**held** after the q = 1/(p·k) fix that the control itself forced.

**What would make it wrong.** (1) Greedy bisection is not provably optimal — it is an
*upper* bound on the floor, so the true idiot index is ≥ my ratios; the claim is
conservative in that direction, and equidistribution is near-optimal for these smooth
integrands. (2) The floor is float-explored; an integer build must place panel edges as
Q61 ψ (the walk's `cut()` already does exactly this) and re-verify the certificate in
integers — the criterion is unchanged (2⁻³⁰ vs the same oracle family), but the knot set
moves, so **this is a canon reset** (digests move; the record already files panel count
as a law change). (3) The crossings table thins with the ladder: `crossing_state` /
`crossing_time` consumers in-tree are benches/examples only (checked: `events::edge`
binary-searches time, the engine never reads crossings), but the exported API's index
granularity changes — a compatibility note, not a physics change. (4) Panels wider than
the shipped ones near periapsis must still keep the Newton solve bracketed; N_cap
enforces CAP everywhere, and the floor's own panels already sit at ≤ CAP at the
high-e tail (measured N_cap = N* at p = 8, +3 elsewhere). **Cost of adoption:** one
focused build (replace the EDGE ladder in `OrbitShape::bound`/`append` with an
error-equidistributed integer edge list computed from the key, keeping `cut()`, CAP and
the quad), then the certificate re-freeze the record already prescribes for any panel-
count change. **Fit with PRINCIPLES:** #1 ("the cheapest compute is compute not done" —
~5–10× of the panels are compute nobody's certificate needs), #16 ("discrete by design,
cheapest arithmetic" — the win is in the grid, not in costlier arithmetic), #21 ("rough
first, refine when a certificate could break" — the ladder is refinement no certificate
demanded); it deletes nothing the physics needs and adds no component.

**The lens** (first principles, one component): the component is the compiled cycle's
quadrature table (generators and certificates). Requirement: answer "where is the body"
and the three increments to the law's 2⁻³⁰ certificate (PRINCIPLES #2: exact means the
certificate holds; error type over size). Floor: N panels of 4-point Gauss — information
the integrands' smoothness actually demands. Idiot index: 4.8–10.5× on the expensive
band, ~1.5× at low e. Design within 10× of the floor: the thinned integer grid (at the
floor, 1.0× by construction). What the lens may have missed: cost moved to the
neighbours — the readout's binary search gets *faster* (fewer knots), the Newton solve
is unchanged (CAP kept), and the per-knot cost is unchanged; the one neighbour not
measured is the *weak branch* (q = 0, periapsis ≥ 2⁶² M), which the grid never reaches
in practice, and the passage/plunge walks (`Passage::compile`, 89–97% of the leaver
rebind), which share the ladder shape and should inherit the same floor analysis before
the build lands.
