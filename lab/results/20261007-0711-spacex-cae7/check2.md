**What the author missed.**
1. The compatibility note "`cargo test -p emergent-universe`: 7 passed / 3 failed identically with and without this patch" regenerates only in debug (plain `cargo test`, no `--release`: 7 passed / 3 failed, the step.rs passage/handoff tests — verified); in `--release` the same suite gives 55 passed / 0 failed / 1 ignored across 10 binaries. The mode was not stated, so a release-first checker sees a contradiction. The substance (patch inert on tests; failures pre-existing in files the patch never touches) is confirmed.
2. The patch silently **deletes the `--level` CLI flag** (the match arm is replaced by the new args; `universe --level 2` now errors "unknown universe argument"). The dossier's cost-of-adoption section calls the probe purely additive. Restore the arm before adopting.
3. The probe's 50 s wall cap guards only the staged loop; the final `settle(target)` is uncapped — belt 6,000 took ~220 s here, and the 20,000 clamp would run ~10+ min on this shared runner, breaking the ≤60 s evidence convention (and the rung rules) exactly at the scales b-h-live will care about. Cap the whole probe or stage the target.
4. `bytes_of` excludes the 224 B Entry slot the catalogue holds per body (and Passage is charged a flat 4,096): true live stock is slightly *higher* than reported — direction favors the claim, but the queue's gate should count it.
5. Minor: the rate×retention product is ~0.083 at 2 AU (3.57e-5 × 2,331 s), not the stated 0.05. Immaterial (both ≪ 1; the mandatory segment dominates either way).
