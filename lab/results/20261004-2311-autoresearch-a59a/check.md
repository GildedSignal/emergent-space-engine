**Abstract.** Claim: `Orbit::compile`'s cost is attributed — cos ≈32% > panel glue ≈24% > sqrt ≈10% > 128-bit division ≈8% — and one exact change (f64-seeded floor sqrt, `isqrt_word`, 18 lines) cuts compile 6.2% tuned / 4.8% held-out with every output bit identical. Fully reproduced: interleaved A/B mins 19621→18456 ns (−5.9%) tuning, 22841→21760 (−4.7%) held-out; all four golden hashes and the 22/28 held-out refusals match exactly; 50/50 tests pass. I confirmed the attribution by dummy-out (cos ≈34%, sqrt ≈9%) and `isqrt_word`'s exactness on 16M+ adversarial points; my own strong-field/near-separatrix corpus is bit-identical. The gain is a fraction, not 10×, and deletes nothing: silver. Gold experiment: route kicks through the existing `QuantizedMemo` (adopt the 18 lines now).

**Reasons.**

1. **Reproduction: exact.** Applied `work/patch.diff` to a clean tree; built stock (example only) and changed binaries; interleaved A/B, min of 5–6 runs. Tuning compile 19621→18456 ns (−5.9% vs claimed −6.2%); held-out 22841→21760 (−4.7% vs −4.8%); rebind −5.0%/−5.3% (claimed −4.5%/−3.7%). All four hashes match the dossier digit for digit and are identical stock↔changed. Refusals 22/28 on both. `cargo test --release -p emergent-space`: 50 passed, 0 failed.
2. **Correctness attack: passed.** `isqrt_word` vs `u64::isqrt` on 6.3M perfect-square neighborhoods (the worst case: at the top of the range `fl(x)` rounds y²−1 up onto y², so the f64 seed lands exactly on the square), 5M random over the full u64, 5M in the guard domain [2^60, 2^62), endpoints incl. 2^62: zero mismatches — none even outside the contract. Code-read confirms the guard is structural, not luck: `x ≤ 0` is refused above the call site, `a = 4k + 2x > 0`, `k ≥ 0`, so `denominator ≤ ONE = 2^62` (itself a perfect square); `word_root` implies `denominator ≥ 2^60`.
3. **Own adversarial corpus: passed.** p ∈ {5, 9, 13, 100, 65537, 524288, 2097152, 8388608}, e ∈ {0, 0.15, 0.55, 0.85, 0.97, 0.99} — bit-identical compile and rebind hashes stock↔changed, identical refusal counts (756 ok / 18 refused), compile −3.8%. No regression anywhere.
4. **Attribution spot-checked, not just trusted.** Dummy-out on the stock tree: cos → 19621→~12890 ns (cos ≈6.7 µs ≈ 34%; claimed 32%); u64::isqrt → ~17860 ns (≈9%; claimed 10%). The headline claim — the folk suspect (128-bit division, ≈5–8%) is *not* the cost — is confirmed by the author's own round 3b and by my two independent dummies bracketing it.
5. **Predictions.** All five held, including the honest band (2–8%; measured 4.7–5.4%) and the kill clause (never triggered). File times: `predictions.md` (2 m) precedes `log.md` (1 m) and `dossier.md`, the only files holding held-out numbers — consistent with pre-registration, but `run.log` is empty, so ordering is supported, not proven (the r3 v3 lesson stands).
6. **Novelty.** No H entry, doc or gate touches `Orbit::compile`'s micro-cost or word-level isqrt; H0210 names "kicks cost 29 µs" and H0211 lists kick cost as open. The named 10× lead is real, not invented: `QuantizedMemo` exists (orbit.rs:874, with hit counters) and `crates/universe/src/bind.rs:185` calls `Rebinding::from_state` per kick. The patch touches no gate, test or criterion; nothing was weakened.
7. **Why not gold.** The rubric demands 10× on a board bottleneck, a deletion, or a killed premise. This is a verified 5–6% on one stage of the 29 µs kick, deletes nothing, and the "premise" it kills (divisions are the cost) is folklore, not a board premise. The author says so honestly.

**The one experiment that would make it gold.** Route `crates/universe`'s per-kick `Rebinding::from_state` through the crate's existing `QuantizedMemo` (12-bit shapes), so most kicks rebind scale-only instead of recompiling a shape that depends only on (k, q). Measured against the engine's own kick bench with the same hash gate: compile is ~65–70% of `from_state`, so removing most of it is the plausible 10× on the rebind stage. The 18-line `isqrt_word` is adoptable now, independently of that.

**What the author missed.**

- The bench's rebind loop `.unwrap()`s: my corpus (legitimate keys) panics with `NoBoundOrbit` inside `from_state`; both shipped corpora happen to contain no refusing states, so the harness is fragile for whoever runs it next.
- `isqrt_word`'s doc comment bounds x < 2^62 but does not say *why* it fails above (the `saturating_mul`/round-to-2^64 hazard near u64's top): a future call site could violate the bound silently. One `debug_assert!` or one sentence would make the invariant load-bearing. (The current call site is structurally safe — verified above.)
- The exactness argument is one line (seed < 2^31, so the f64 error is < 10⁻³ units; ±1 correction always terminates) but is asserted via IEEE correctness instead of proved.
- No per-key latency table: corpus minima could hide a single-key regression; my stronger-field corpus also improved, so none is evident, but the author never showed it.
- Attribution rows for panel glue (24%), cut/bands and loop (never dummy-checked); I verified the two biggest levers only.
