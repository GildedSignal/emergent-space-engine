**What the author missed** (none changes a metric or the verdict):

- The sd16 kick-bias number and mechanism are wrong: "measured bias −2.2×10⁻¹²·v₀/kick" from sub-half-LSB kicks. Measured over all 128 real streams the mean residue is **positive** everywhere, +3.5×10⁻¹³ … +2.3×10⁻¹² (median +7.8×10⁻¹³). Mechanism (Monte Carlo, 2×10⁸ samples): rounding log-uniform magnitudes at the dyadic grid has mean residue +0.014…0.026·F, which couples to the sign bias E[s] ≈ 0.092 → +0.092·0.016·F ≈ +7×10⁻¹³·v₀, matching measurement; the sub-LSB drift-loss story predicts the wrong sign (−1.3×10⁻¹²) and is not the dominant term. Conclusions survive: |bias|·10⁶ ≈ 8×10⁻⁷·v₀ ≪ the 1% band (drift passes), and the sd16 residual is still linear (type failure).
- Worst sd16 phase error is 1.4–1.6×10⁻³ rad (the author's own detail.json says 1.394×10⁻³; true max over seeds 1.572×10⁻³), not "4×10⁻³".
- "16.7% of kicks below half an LSB" and "max kick = 0.03 LSB" are top-of-octave bounds; over the ensemble they range 9–17% and 0.03–0.066 (a body's position inside its binary octave sets F/v₀).
- The aside "a global least-squares fit reads 2.4 on pure √N data" is loose: single-path OLS on synthetic walks reads −0.1…0.24 here. The substantive point stands — worst-seed fits of *any* estimator read 1–2.5 on sub-linear data (my ensemble max 1.93; OLS-all on the sd16 worst path 2.5).
- Not tested (author's own list): second-order modulation, non-dyadic grids. Both are irrelevant to the conclusions: no modulator order recovers sub-F input information (finite-state argument the dossier makes), and non-dyadic grids cannot make 2w bits hold 53-bit floats.
