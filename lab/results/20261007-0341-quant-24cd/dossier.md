# Dossier — run 20261007-0341-quant-24cd (lens: quant)

**Claim.** On the plane as shipped, frame cost is member-flat — 4×10⁵ fleet members ride at rtf 100 with frame p99 ≈ 11 ms and no hitch — because the engine's budget converts its true ~30 ms p99 tail into a 27–30% *silent fleet deferral* that `rtf_median` (always exactly 100.0) does not see; the throughput knee is in rtf, not members (rtf floor 77–310 at ≥3,072 members, rtf 10⁴ held at 48), and the player's burn costs less than a coast frame (0.73–0.93×), while the player's own doll advance is the single most expensive item (1.9 ms per eval, 30× a ship's).

**Bottleneck.** Board #4 (smoothness and the allocator) and plane.md's "measured on the whole": where the smooth 100× breaks, which stage crosses first, and what the frame budget hides. Orders of magnitude, Linux/x86, 1 thread, load 0.8–1.9 recorded per run; the integrator re-measures on the M1.

## Evidence (all runs `xtask universe --bench`, ≤ 60 s each, seed 1 unless noted; JSON + per-frame CSV in this folder)

Reproduce any row: `bash grid.sh <tag> <members> <rtf> <seconds> <engine_ms> <seed>`; summaries: `python3 summary.py`, per-frame: `python3 analyze.py <tag>`. The per-frame CSV needs the 20-line `PLANE_FRAME_DUMP` patch to `xtask/src/universe/mod.rs` (in `patch.diff`; without it the summary JSONs still print).

1. **Members do not price the frame (sparse causal order, measured on the plane).** At rtf 100, budget 5 ms, 6 s: members 768 / 12,288 / 49,152 / 98,304 / 393,216 give frame p99 = 10.3 / 10.1 / 10.2 / 11.1 / 11.0 ms, engine p99 5.5–5.8 (budget-pinned), view p99 4.0–4.7, ev/s 2.15–2.26e3, worst hitch 11.5–13.0 ms, zero frames above 16.7 ms. The engine's *stats are identical across 64× members* (slices 6,247–6,315, kicks 3,434–3,470, encounters 68–69, leavers 374): cost tracks events, not bodies × ticks. Uncapped (`--engine-ms 500`, 3 s): c768 ≡ c12288 ≡ c49152 (slices 4,304, engine p99 ≈ 30 ms, ev/s 3.21e3). Principle 14 ("no work for no reason, so no ticks") is not a hope; it is the measured cost law of the flying plane.
2. **The budget is a measured smoother, and its price is deferral the dashboard's rtf cannot see.** True engine demand p99 ≈ 30 ms (uncapped) presents as frame p99 10.2, p99.9 12.7, no 16.7 ms hitch — but overflow frames are 26–30% from 768 members up, and the fleet resolves at 1,052 slices/game-s against the 1,435 required (uncapped rate): **73% resolution while `rtf_median` reads exactly 100.0** (same at 393k members: 70%). The paced service-level run at 49k members (10 s at 60 Hz) confirms it end to end: frame p50 4.97 / p99 10.44 / worst 10.7 ms, zero hitches, rtf_median exactly 100.0 — and 978 slices/game-s, **68% resolution** (run `p49k`, paced: `PLANE_FRAME_DUMP=frames-p49k.csv xtask universe --bench --members 49152 --rtf 100 --seconds 10`). `overflow_frames` is printed but no service-level number converts it; a reader of the dashboard sees "100×, no hitch". Open (not measured here): whether the gather shows the lagged battle to the player (the fleet is 20 km away — no slack excuse) or the cache hides it. One-line fix candidate: report resolution-rate ratio and deferral p99 next to rtf.
3. **The knee is rtf, not members — and it is not 1/members.** 48 members: rtf 10,000 holds (overflow 112/240). 3,072 members: floor 77 (target 10³), 155 (target 10⁴), overflow 240/240. 49,152: floor 310 at both targets. Non-monotonic in members on seed 1 — the floor is set by the battle's phase and event mix, not a members law; one seed per point, so no exponent is claimed. ev/s saturates at 2.2–3.2e3 single-thread (capped/uncapped).
4. **The player order is free; the player's doll is dear (lens #6).** Per-frame CSV, burn windows (n % 240 < 20) vs coast: engine p99 ratio 0.73 / 0.91 / 0.93 / 0.73 at 48 / 768 / 49,152 members / rtf 1,000; worst frames scatter on coast frames. But `engine_cost_by_kind`: player 1.88–1.9 ms per eval, ship 63–68 µs, fleet slice 40–43 µs. The wildcard's price is his doll's bookkeeping (kick windows), 30× a ship's — consistent with gold components-4643 (window setup dominates) and the un-wired kick memo (H0203 note: the plane recompiles on ~99% of kicks).
5. **Early-run bursts exist even tiny:** uncapped 48-member run has single engine frames of 13.5 ms and 7.2 ms (frames 62, 11; fleet contact wave and handouts) — steady state is clean, the first seconds are not; paced runs will carry these unless the budget eats them (it did: worst presented 6.4 ms at 48 members).

## Predictions (predictions.md, written before the grid)

- P1 (engine binds first) **wrong in an informative way**: neither the engine (budget-pinned, member-flat) nor the view (flat 4.0–4.7 ms p99 from 768 to 393,216) binds in members; the frame is engine-budget + view, both flat, to 4×10⁵.
- P2 (throughput-bound, no hitches, budget defers) **held** exactly: p99.9 ≤ 13 ms everywhere, zero 16.7/33/50 ms frames in 15 runs.
- P3 (cost ∝ ticks × members) **wrong — better**: cost ∝ events, and events are member-flat at fixed resolution progress; the rtf floor is real but not ∝ 1/members (P3's own falsifier fired: the floor product is not constant).
- P4 (burn ≤ 1.5× coast) **held with margin** (≤ 0.93×).
- P5 (knee in [10³, 10⁵] members at rtf 100) **held only if "knee" means deferral-free**: overflow < 5% up to ~200 members, 26–30% from 768 up; in *presented* rtf there is no members knee at all within 4×10⁵.

## What would make it wrong

Linux/x86, 1 thread, load 0.8–1.9, cold caches each run: orders of magnitude only (README); the M1 may shift the members-flat margin (bandwidth is wider, budget share differs) but the mechanism (budget → deferral, cost ∝ events) is representation, not silicon. The deferral reading assumes the uncapped run's 1,435 slices/game-s is the requirement; if the fleet's lazy cone legitimately needs less at rtf 100, deferral is slack, not lateness — deciding that needs the view-side check named above, which I did not run. The rtf-floor points are single seeds (seed variation at 49k members, rtf 100: overflow 91–100 across seeds 1–3, frame p99 10.2–11.7 — tight; floors untested across seeds).

## Cost of adopting, and fit

Adoption is an instrument change plus one decision: report resolution-rate ratio (and its p99) beside `rtf_median` in the dashboard, and treat deferral, not rtf, as the capacity number (a ~20-line change in `crates/universe/src/dash.rs` + the report — crates I did not touch; the lab rule). Nothing else in the engine changes: the member-flatness and free-player-order results *delete* work — no members-based capacity scaling, no decision debouncing, no view culling for members; the lever the numbers point at is the one the board already holds (cheaper kick windows: 64 µs ship, 1.9 ms player evals). PRINCIPLES: "no ticks" (14) and "compute only surprises" (3) measured as the plane's actual cost law; "the algorithm that allocates the work… is the most important one" (OC#36) shown converting a 30 ms tail into deferral — which "the goal, never a proxy" (27) says must then be *measured*, not hidden.

## The lens (what quant may have missed)

HFT eyes price tails and throughput; they under-price *semantic* lag — deferral is invisible precisely because every latency instrument I trusted reads green. The checker should attack the deferral claim first (is the uncapped rate really the requirement?), and re-run one members row on a warm, loaded machine (I ran sequential, load recorded, but the box is shared). The M1's 8 GB and fanless heat cap the members claim long before x86 did (393k bodies ≈ 120 MB here) — memory, not time, will price members on the target machine.
