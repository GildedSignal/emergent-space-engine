Grade: silver

**Abstract.** Claim: the kick path is compute-bound (made 36–38 µs, leavers 92–102 µs, flat as the store passes L3, record clone 0.14% of a kick, exact-memo hit 0.0), and between 400 and 4,000 fleet members the flight dies on a "0.95 s unsplittable fleet battle slice" plus a 14.9 ms view gather. Every digest reproduces bit-identically and every number within machine noise — but the 0.95 s is misattributed: instrumenting the run shows one slice of 20 ms plus 2,091 hand-out events of 443 µs (927 ms), already split across frames by the 5 ms budget. The real levers are hand-out cost (12× a kick) and deadline pinning, not slice granularity. Gain: deletes record-packing and clone-elimination as levers; renames the fleet-scale wall. Adoption: correct the target, build nothing.

**Reasons.**

1. **Reproduction (clean tree, patch applied, i7-4790, same shared target dir).** Sizes test exact (`BodyRec=448 … Tube=16`). Kick sweep: made medians 35.3/37.0/36.7 µs at 71/393/3,842 bodies (author 41.8/36.2/38.2), leavers 120.2/93.1/102.1 µs, clone 0.044–0.056 µs, exact hit 0.0, quantized hit 0.832/0.910/0.945 — the quantized rates and all four digests and replay digests match bit for bit. Bench m4000: frame p50 29.1 ms, gather p50 14.89 ms, hitches 198,000/h, rtf 0, presented tick 32, `cost_ns[4]` 955 ms on `items[4]=1`, digest identical. maxrss matches the author's `.err` files (40 MB / 590 MB; 1.55 GB and 3.5 GB for the 40k/200k deaths). The warm-path flatness past L3 (2.8→34.5 MB, made 1.06×) is confirmed as far as it goes.

2. **Predictions.** `predictions.md` (11:09:52) precedes the first sweep JSON (11:13:42); scoring is honest (P3, P4 reported as failed). Two of six held-out predictions failed, which alone bars gold.

3. **The headline new wall is a misattribution — checked by instrumenting `resolve_fleet` and the hand-out path, then restoring the tree.** `cost_ns[4]` has two write sites: the fleet slice (universe.rs:658) *and every hand-out* (universe.rs:597, "The fleet's cost (a hand-out was inside its item): time, not an item"). At m4000 the 180 frames contain exactly one `resolve_fleet` call, **20.4 ms**, plus **2,091 hand-out events totalling 926.9 ms at 443 µs each** — 97.9% of the "0.95 s". The budget already splits this work across frames (~12 hand-outs per frame; engine p50 5.4 ms = the 5 ms budget spent on them), exactly as the design intends (universe.rs comment cites plane.md foundation 7). Nothing is unsplittable. What pins the flight (presented tick 32, 180/180 overflow, zero kicks) is ~2,100 mandatory events whose deadlines the presented time cannot pass, at 443 µs each — a burst-drain and per-event-cost problem, not a granularity problem. "Making the fleet slice splittable" buys 20 ms, not the flight. The "57 frames' worth of budget" arithmetic also uses the 16.7 ms frame where the engine budget is 5 ms (honest figure ≈ 190 frames, of ~2,092 events). The stall itself is real, new and reproduced.

4. **Novelty.** The gather wall is on record since H0210 ("the view gather is 83% of a light frame"), with a cache added in H0211; 14.9 ms at 2,132 bodies is a re-measurement at a new scale, not a discovery. The exact-memo 0% is H0213/the 5 October correction — the dossier flags this itself. Kick cost ~29–38 µs is H0210's number re-measured; the made/leaver split, the L3 flatness and the scenario-build memory superlinearity are new detail. The redirect that deletes record packing and clone elimination as levers is sound and adoptable.

5. **Goal fit.** Smoothness/allocator is a board bottleneck, and "the cheapest compute is compute not done" applies to the 443 µs hand-out as much as to the kick; but the run names the wrong component for the fleet-scale half.

**The one experiment that would make it gold.** Re-derive the fleet-scale wall from the hand-out burst: why one hand-out (bind a tube, plan the first interval) costs 443 µs — 12× a made-ship kick — and whether batching, amortising or re-deadlining the burst lets the presented time advance; measure across seeds, `Order` and thread counts. A 10× cheaper drain that restores flight at ≥ 4,000 members (with the gather's 14.9 ms addressed or shown non-binding) is gold.

**What the author missed.**
- The contradiction sat in the author's own JSON: `items[4]=1`, `leavers: 2104`, `cost_ns[4]=951 ms` — one "event" cannot cost 0.95 s while 2,104 leavers appear; the code comment at universe.rs:597 says the counter mixes them. This is the 5 October lesson (check that the measured events are distinct) repeated from the other side.
- Even at zero engine cost, the m4000 frame is ~23.5 ms (gather 14.9 + json 7.1 + wire 1.5): the flight cannot hold 60 Hz at 2,132 bodies regardless of the engine — stated nowhere.
- The m400 "fleet 88.8 µs/item" is contaminated the same way (361 hand-outs folded into 2,672 slice items).
- Made-kick statistics rest on n=50 (≈23 made ships exist); the flatness claim is the warm-path floor only, self-flagged, leaving the DRAM regime ≥ 10⁴ bodies — the regime the billion-body question actually lives in — unmeasured.
- The registered sweep sizes ({48, 480, 4800, 48000, 240000}; real path {48, 48000}) differ from the ones run ({48, 400, 4000, 40000, 200000}; {400, 4000}), unremarked.
- The 200k run breached the window's 2 GB budget (3.5 GB) — disclosed, but it also means the memory-superlinearity curve ends in a killed run, not a measurement.
