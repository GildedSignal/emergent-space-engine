Grade: silver

**Abstract.** Claim: the shipped event queue (4-ary heap, Scalar keys) is 4-8x off its workload's floor; a monotone bucket wheel is 2.1-4.4x cheaper per event (4-9x under co-orbital concentration), lower p99, at the cost of rare O(n) rebuild stalls. Reproduced in a clean tree: all grid cells match (wheel at 10^6 within +/-6%: 179/68/219/184 vs 188/69/224/187 ns; 10^7 wheel 318 vs 343; ratios 2.2-6.6x on a quieter box); pop sequences verified identical to the shipped Calendar on 14 configurations incl. cancels. Four adversarial workloads (re-set storms at 1/8 and 1/4, bimodal two-clock, same-tick volley) all verified exact and the wheel still won 1.5-6.4x. Cost to adopt: swap `crates/space` Calendar internals (~150 lines, interface kept) or first just key on u64 ticks (1.2-1.7x). Not gold: gain is 2-9x on the component, the queue is not a ranked board bottleneck, and the engine-level share ("queue's share of a 32-kick orbit drops ~3x") is asserted, never measured.

PRINCIPLES-respecting.

**Why not gold.** The 10x bar is not met on the component (2-9x), no component is deleted, no premise is killed, and the load-bearing claim for the board — the queue's share of the engine's real cost — is asserted ("drops ~3x") without an in-engine measurement. Kicks dominate the engine's cost (board); if the queue's slice is small, the end-to-end gain is proportionally small. One number the log contradicts: the dossier's "24 ms at 10^6" rebuild spike is the formation burst; belt 10^6 measures 90-130 ms in the author's own `metrics.jsonl` and 80-105 ms in mine. The stall claim (rare, amortized <1 ns/event, chunkable under plane foundation 7) survives, but the number as written is wrong.

**The one experiment to gold.** Wire the wheel (or even only u64 keys) behind the shipped queue's interface and measure the plane's own cadence end-to-end (`behave --law plane`, or the kick loop at 10^6 bodies): show the queue's slice before/after on the engine, not the harness, and demonstrate the chunked rebin that bounds the stall. If the slice is >=10% of per-event cost and drops >=3x, that is adoptable-in-one-step gold on board item 3; if the slice is ~1%, the honest grade is a cheap known win (u64 keys), not a queue rewrite.

**What the author missed.**
- The 24 ms/10^6 misattribution above (belt vs formation).
- The Scalar/u64 comparison charges the bench's own `tick_of` conversion on every scalar pop; the engine keys on Scalar natively, so 1.2-1.7x is an upper bound (the 16-vs-8-byte compare share is real but unseparated).
- No measurement under the engine's actual concurrency (ties.rs runs the queue across 1-8 threads); side-heap and bucket drains are presumably no worse than heap sifts, but nothing shown.
- The `b = min(n, 2^22)` bucket cap means beyond ~4M bodies width grows with n; visible at 10^7 (disclosed as cold headers) but its scaling to 10^9 — the argument the dossier itself calls "the only term that must survive" — is asserted from 10^7, not measured.
- Bimodal scenes concentrate half a million entries in one bucket (38 ms sort): the batch-spike family is broader than formation's; worth a sentence in any adoption note.
