# Dossier — 20261013-2252-dynamics-ea91

## Claim

On the shipped plane the surprise-rate distribution across bodies is heavy-tailed and its
ranks are frozen by regime (natives ρ = 0.98–1.0, live promoted ρ = 0.85–0.93, at or above
the counting-noise null) — the rank rule (nladder #2) survives its falsifier — but the
churn channel is **death**: 54–64 % of promotions crash within one run window, and the
steady stream avalanches (Fano 8–28, top 1 % of frames carry 10–20 % of items).

## Bottleneck

The furthest-gap one (board): bodies in the flyable engine, 28 against 10⁹. Open direction
1 of [lab/wiki/nladder.md](../../wiki/nladder.md): the per-body surprise-rate distribution
was never measured; rule 2 ("the live set is a rank") stood on an assumption. This run
measures it and splits the tail by mechanism. Gain: no 10× compute claim — it deletes the
open question of *what the promotion certificate must track* (rank over a live census, with
death as the exit channel), and it prices the sticky-promotion risk: a majority of
promotions are one-burst-then-gone, so standing state must be O(compact), never O(live).

## Evidence

Env-gated probe on shipped code (`ES_SURPRISE=1`): per-body resolved-item counters in
`Universe::apply` (never read by the engine; digests bit-identical with the probe off and
on, checked on every scene), plus the public `enc_log`, per-frame event deltas and a
half-run census snapshot in the bench report. Scenes (all `--unpaced`, canonical cut,
deterministic digests): `tune` = seed 1, m48, rtf 100, 30 s; held-out `ho1` = seed 3,
m400, rtf 100, 55 s; `ho2` = seed 7, m48, rtf 100, 55 s; `ho3` = seed 3, m48, rtf 1, 30 s.
All regenerated by `regen.sh` (~20 s warm, ~6 min cold build).

Rank stability, exposure-fair rates, halves of one run, **live bodies only**:
natives ρ = 1.0 / 0.98 / 0.98 (n = 28); live promoted ρ = 0.55 / 0.85 / 0.93 against each
scene's Poisson re-noise null 0.78 / 0.72 / 0.85. Include the dead and ρ collapses to
−0.05…0.48 with top-decile Jaccard 0.08–0.17: **the apparent churn is bodies ceasing to
exist, not quiet bodies being passed**. The player (417 items) is the busiest or
second-busiest body everywhere (a promoted ship beats it at ho1, 519 vs 417).

**The aggregate stream (per-frame resolved events at the player's flying pace):** Fano
63.1 at m400, 11.9–13.2 at m48; steady tail (last 30 % of frames, after the promotion
transient dies): Fano 7.6–28.1, top 1 % of frames carry 10.0–19.9 % of items, 82–83 %
zero-frames at m48/m400, lag-1 autocorrelation ≈ 0.05 (no linear memory: avalanches arrive
unannounced). The first two deciles hold 55–70 % of all items — the fleet empties into
dolls early and every promotion bursts at birth (total items *anti*-correlate with age,
ρ = −0.19…−0.52), then settles to 1–3 items per half-run. Control: at rtf 1 (`ho3`, 30
game-s) only 57 items exist and the stream is regular (Fano 0.03) — the plane's live event
stream is thin at battle pace; every distribution number above is a flying-pace number.

Commands (each ≤ 60 s): `bash lab/results/20261013-2252-dynamics-ea91/regen.sh` runs all
four scenes and prints the table; `python3 lab/results/20261013-2252-dynamics-ea91/analyze.py`
regenerates `analysis.json` from the saved `census-*.json`.

## Predictions

[predictions.md](predictions.md) was written before the matrix ran (the 5 s sanity run
only checked the instrument and digest-neutrality). Scorecard: P1 **half-held** — Gini
≥ 0.6 and top-decile ≥ 50 % held (0.715–0.737, 47–56 %), but the Zipf slope is *steeper*
than −1 (−1.6…−2.0): heavy in the concentration sense, finite-variance, not Extremistan.
P2 **half-held** — the player is top-1 or top-2, and the native spread is regime-frozen,
but a promoted ship outranks the player at ho1. P3 **held among the living** (0.85–0.93 at
n ≥ 42) and **falsified as written** (ρ ≥ 0.8 including the dead: −0.05…0.48) — the
refinement is the run's main finding: churn = death. P4 **held** (Fano 8–63 ≫ 2); the
encounter-vs-kick churn comparison was **untestable**: on the plane the encounter tier is
player-centric by construction (`PlayerNeighbourhood`), so only 2–5 bodies ever appear in
`enc_log`. P5 **held** (top-decile within ±10 points across seeds/members; the rtf 1 arm
too sparse to test — disclosed above).

## What would make it wrong

The death channel is the battle scene's (members flung into the planet; frame-cost already
names that density "a local pocket no 10⁹ can live in") — a quiet-belt promotion mix could
die less and dormant more; then the demote-at-commit lever weakens and retirement matters
less. Two bins bound the correlation time from above only: ranks are stable at the
half-run scale (≈ 1,500–2,750 game-s), not measured below it. Zipf slope and Gini at
n ≤ 428 carry small-sample error (±0.1). The census counts every `apply`, so a death is
itself one item; excluded where it matters (alive-only tables). Adoption cost: none — this
is a measurement; the design consequence lands in the promotion rule (nladder #2/#4) and
the allocator's burst absorption (PRINCIPLES #12).

Fit with PRINCIPLES: "Compute only surprises" — the surprise census is exactly the object
priced; "the simulation decides; heuristics schedule" — the promotion certificate must
read death and burst structure from the simulation, not a fixed fraction; "no time
estimates… a step whose builder passes no new gate" — this is a gate input, not a build.

## The lens

A dynamics lens looked for the attractor and found a transient wearing one's clothes: the
promotion burst then quiescence, and the population stream settling after the fleet
empties. What it may have missed: the *spatial* structure (is activity a travelling wave
with the player? the encounter tier's player-centricity hid it), and the belt-scale
regime where none of this was measured. Rank stability below the half-run scale needs a
finer census (quarter-windows), one env var away.
