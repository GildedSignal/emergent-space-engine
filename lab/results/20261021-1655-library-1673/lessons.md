# Five lessons for the family's adoption decision (each holds one decision)

1. **Egan, *Diaspora*** — "it required a profound myopia of scale and similarity to believe
   that everything beyond this shallow layer could be ignored."
   Mechanism: representations are scale-bound; 033c's r²·H death law makes the family's
   domain a scale fact (word forks in 3–5 kicks at 47,400 root, 1–20 at 150,000), not a
   population choice. Decision: no gate flight is owed for the Home/Moon locals (11.6–17.3×
   beyond the kill radius) or GEO child dolls; the family's flights go to the belt and the
   fleet lattice only. Test: none needed at the dead radii — the anchors are flown.

2. **Munger** — "To a man with only a hammer every problem looks pretty much like a nail."
   Mechanism: one shared-store route applied everywhere taxes each population for another's
   physics: the phase-locked belt's cheapest store is the compact one (2.6–5 B) — the
   family floors at its word w = 8–32 B and misses the ≤ 6 B row at every F. Decision: the
   family is not the 10⁹ belt store; its bytes row is the driven/fleet population, where it
   beats the compact store's own worst case 1.44× (10.9 vs 15.75 B at F = 400, w = 8).
   Test: one wired arm at the belt must lose to the wired compact store — that loss is the
   certificate that the split is by population, not preference.

3. **Taleb** — "The more frequently you look at data, the more noise you are disproportionally
   likely to get (rather than the valuable part called the signal); hence the higher the
   noise to signal ratio." Mechanism: the per-kick disagree census and the first-fork
   integer are the highest-frequency looks (033c's own phase lottery moved it 682→3,367 at
   fixed dx); the certificate decision rides the steady stream rate. Decision: od 4's
   experiment measures steady re-sync rates, never first-fork integers; the fork bill is
   priced from the rate. Test: the worst recurring rate (1 fork/35 orbits) at the dearest
   handler (58 µs) is 0.22% of the stream saving — already composed here.

4. **Housel, *The Psychology of Money*** — "Growth is driven by compounding, which always
   takes time. Destruction is driven by single points of failure, which can happen in
   seconds." Mechanism: the (tick, id) calendar key is the single point every sharing route
   fails at (b560's code read: every body its own entry); merging it is digest-neutral on
   the aligned grid (b560 bit-identical through 41,328 co-fires) and compounds fires and
   interns for every route at once. Decision: the adoption sequence starts with the one
   calendar-item change, before w11 and before any gate. Test: a key-merge arm that holds
   the digest at F = 16 on the aligned grid is the whole proof.

5. **Taleb, *The Black Swan*** — "In Extremistan, inequalities are such that one single
   observation can disproportionately impact the aggregate, or the total." Mechanism: the
   event ledger is regime-split — the belt is Mediocristan (1.76e-7 ev/B/game-s at the
   floor; 10⁹ of them = 0.0044× budget) while LEO-cadence fleet bodies are Extremistan
   (2.75e-3 ev/B/game-s, 1.6e4× the belt's): 1–10% fleet share swings the whole 10⁹ kick
   row 0.69×→6.9× budget. Decision: the family's F× fire collapse is spent where one
   population dominates the total — the fleet (44.6× at the plane's own 48 members;
   closure at any share with F = 10⁴) — never averaged into the belt's row.
   Test: compose the 10⁹ ledger with the fleet share as its own row; 5% share must flip
   it from closed (1.3× under at 0% share… 0.69× at 1%) to 3.4× over.
