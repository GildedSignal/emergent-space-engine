# Dossier — inversion-b1c0: the adopted k=4 train certificate misses encounters in every crowded scene, governed by a number the design does not measure

## Claim (one sentence)
The phase-neighbour certificate adopted from gold `20261006-0346-taleb-09b5` (rank-4 watch, "zero misses", triggered by the all-Full Binet condition — build spec `lab/lenses/b-coorbital-cert.md`) is not a certificate outside the sparse corner it was measured in: the miss is governed by along-track crowding J = ρ/spacing (rank_max ≈ 1 + (1.2–1.7)·J, miss rate 19%→78% as J goes 5→87), it returns to quadratic work in the blob limit (rank doubles when n doubles at fixed extent), the stated trigger never fires anywhere (19–461× above threshold, including the gold's own validated phalanx), and its "rebuild on overtakes" maintenance is 100–700 events per body per orbit, not a rare Diophantine event.

## Bottleneck
The board's top scaling risk (candidate work must track real encounters) — the co-orbital certificate was the adopted answer for concentrated workloads. This run does not kill the train idea; it kills its **zero-miss claim at fixed k and its stated trigger**: the certificate holds only at J ≲ 1, and the scenes that break it (a jammed fleet — spacing below the encounter radius) are exactly the battle formations the game wants. Expected gain of acting on it: the builder of `b-coorbital-cert.md` does not ship a k=4 watch with a never-firing trigger; what replaces it is measured here (see "adoption").

G-uniform (their fleet): rank 1 everywhere. e-spread ×10 (B): rank still ≤ 3, J unchanged — radial spread is *not* the binding invariant (the gold's own suspicion, now measured).
2. **The crowding law** (held-out): rank_long_max = 11, 19, 52, 101, 24, 3 at J50 = 5.0, 14.7, 44.4, 87.4, 13.7, 2.8 → ≈ 1 + (1.15–1.7)·J for J ≥ 5 (Poisson clumping puts the constant above 1 at small J: A-phalanx J=0.76 → rank 3). k=4 misses encounters in **every** held-out scene: 559/2958 (19%), 2984/6065 (49%), 5249/8575 (61%), 25509/32729 (78%), 1229/5004 (25%), 0/65.
3. **The quadratic return**: E-blob (n=400, extent ~10ρ) → F (n=800, same extent): rank_long_max 52 → 101 (×1.94 for ×2 bodies). Train cost n·k ∝ n² at fixed extent — the ruin state (40 TB / core-years arithmetic in the gold) comes back through the k term, not the pair term.

The "rebuild on overtakes, a Diophantine event" cost is O(100–1000) per body per orbit — an O(n) per-orbit term the design must budget, not a rare event.

## Predictions
Written before the held-out run (`predictions.md`). All six held, including the kill criterion direction (no scene with J > 1 kept rank ≤ 4). One parameter error, honestly: I predicted I-highe's J ≈ 15 from σ_λ alone, but σ_e = 3e-2 fattens true-longitude spread (equation of centre ±2e), making the *effective* J = 2.8 — and the law still held there (rank 3 ≈ 1+J), which strengthens it: crowding must be measured in the coordinate the sort uses (true longitude), not assigned from σ_λ.

## What would make it wrong
Rank is measured at each encounter's own closest-approach time against all bodies at that instant — if the real certificate is allowed to watch a different key (e.g. mean longitude instead of true longitude) the constant changes but not the law (the between-count is set by linear density × contact window). All scenes are co-orbital, prograde, 2-D, two-body-unperturbed truth at one a-band — the same domain as the gold's evidence, so this cannot speak to mixed fleet+belt seams, retrogrades, or perturbed fleets. The overtake count is exact per pair on a 64-body iid subsample, scaled ×(n choose 2)/(64 choose 2); ±30% on the scaled number is possible, not the orders of magnitude at stake. The gold's own miss bound was never proven (their words: "it needs a proof that co-orbital sub-ρ contact implies rank ≤ k under stated invariants (radial spread ≲ ρ·c), which I did not write") — this run supplies the counterexample territory: the invariant that actually binds is along-track spacing, not radial spread.

## Cost of adopting, fit with PRINCIPLES
Nothing here ships as engine code; the adoption is a correction to a design about to be built (`b-coorbital-cert.md` is in the queue, GLM and Codex both). What to build instead, in cost order: (1) keep the train at J ≤ 1 but make k adaptive, k = ⌈1 + 3J⌉ — measured to cover the max in every scene here, still cheap until J ~ 10, but O(n·k) ∝ n² in the blob, so only a half-fix; (2) in the blob limit (clump along-track extent ≲ few ρ) do not search pairs at all — certify the clump as a persistent-contact group, O(n) membership, contacts implicit: the failure regime is also the cheapest one ("the best part is no part", #17); (3) the regime number is J = ρ/s measured in true longitude, and it must gate the switch, since the Binet/all-Full trigger never fires (#4: "regimes are chosen by dimensionless numbers" — but the number must be the one that governs the failure). "A fix signals the wrong representation" (#19): a fixed-k neighbour watch is a patch on the pair representation; the jam is a different fish. "Compute only surprises" (#3): a jammed fleet's surprises are group-scale, not pair-scale.

## The lens (what inversion made me miss)
Inversion found the failure and its law but not a shipped alternative: the group certificate in (2) is argued, not measured, and the seam cases (fleet inside a dilute belt, retrograde co-orbitals, a fleet crossing its own resonance) are untouched. A speed lens would have measured the O(n·k) cost curve directly; a red-team would attack my truth's 1-orbit horizon (ranks at contact are horizon-independent, but which pairs encounter is not). The single-orbit horizon and the 64-body overtake subsample are the two places a checker should push.
