# Five lessons from Julien's library — bottleneck: candidate work at scale
All quotes verbatim from his Readwise export (cached: `data/readwise.json`). Probe: `probe.py`, `probe2.py` (7.4 s, 10.4 s). "Candidates" = orbit-crossing pairs, the cheap necessary condition every tier shares.

1. **Invert, always invert** — "Invert, always invert." (Charlie Munger, The Revised Psychology of Human Misjudgment). *Mechanism:* the forward question ("who is near whom") costs O(N²) per look; the inverted one ("when do *these two* next align") is closed-form per pair and paid once per element change. *Measured:* at fixed extent, candidates/body track N with slope 1.0015 (333k partners at 10⁶) — inversion alone doesn't kill the wall, but it changes what you pay: entries, not scans. *Change:* the only entry into encounter work is the per-pair phase-window prediction; every scan path is deleted. *Test:* log ops that changed a prediction vs. ops that didn't, one bench minute; second number must be 0.

2. **News with no predictive ability is pure cost** — "(It takes a huge investment in introspection to learn that the thirty or more hours spent 'studying' the news last month neither had any predictive ability during your activities of that month nor did it impact your current knowledge of the world." (Taleb, Fooled by Randomness). *Mechanism:* a rescan of unchanged candidates updates no prediction; standing predictions cost nothing until falsified. *Change:* no periodic rebuild of candidate sets, ever; payment only on window expiry or element change (Julien: "we only care about changes"). *Test:* same log as lesson 1 — a rebuild that finds zero expired windows is the smell.

3. **Every sensor has a carrying cost** — "Removing radar and ultrasonic sensors and relying solely on vision may make the perception problem easier… The full cost of sensors includes not just their implementation, but also procurement, maintenance, and incorporation into the system." (Andrej Karpathy, Lex Fridman podcast — episode AI notes, so paraphrase-grade). *Mechanism:* each standing structure (tier, memo, skin, calendar) costs maintenance per body per orbit; N structures at O(1) each still lose to one. *Measured sizing law for whatever survives:* candidates/body ≈ ρ_band·⟨a(eᵢ+eⱼ)⟩ — slope 0.93 in a, 0.97 in σ_e, 1.00 in N at fixed extent. *Change:* the encounters layer ships ONE structure (phase-neighbour certificate + its calendar); the rest are deleted, not tuned. *Test:* carrying-cost ledger (ops/body/orbit) per structure on one trace; keep the cheapest that misses nothing.

4. **Redundancy is the budget, not the waste** — "Redundancy is ambiguous because it seems like a waste if nothing unusual happens. Except that something unusual happens—usually." (Taleb, Antifragile). *Mechanism:* p99 smoothness is bought with idle capacity sized to the tail — but the belt's own Kepler statistics never make one: conjunction load is Poisson (burst index 0.95–1.21) and a fleet's conjunction load is 0.28× a hot belt's at the same a. *Change:* size the slack skin from measured game-trace tails (fleets arriving, player-correlated events), not from orbital statistics; fill it in idle time ("Use idle time to compute ahead"). *Test:* replay the bench with the skin sized by Poisson vs. by trace p99; compare worst frame.

5. **Man-masses: pay per band, not per pair** — "Psychohistory dealt not with man, but man-masses. It was the science of mobs; mobs in their billions… The reaction of one man could be forecast by no known mathematics; the reaction of a billion is something else again." (Asimov, Foundation and Empire). *Mechanism:* while group envelopes are disjoint the aggregate prediction is exact; individual entries are only needed where it can break. Not averaging — every body stays real; the group is a certificate, cashed on surprise. *Measured:* dilution (extent grows at fixed density) inflates pair entries ~n/ln n (slope 0.58) while real encounters per body stay flat — the pair tier can never survive dilution; band certificates that pay once can. *Change:* the second tier is bands sized by ρ·a·σ_e, dissolved on surprise, never per-pair at distance. *Test:* two-layer entry count at 10⁶ vs. pair-only; must flatten the dilution slope.
