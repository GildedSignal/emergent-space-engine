# Dossier — 20261019-1452-library-e181 (library lens)

**Claim.** The library's delete-first rule (Musk's algorithm, with its 10% add-back bar)
prices the nladder's named-but-unflown fence: expiring held scans past 16 game-s deletes
the burn-mode memory ratchet (1,600 MB guard-stop at 29.6k game-s → 702 MB flat through a
42% longer flight) at a measured deletion price of ≤ 10 lost encounters in 3,986 (≤ 0.3%)
— the dropped scans are ~35× emptier than the answered ones, so the fence drops exactly
the dead tail.

**Bottleneck.** The board's first (bodies 28 → 10⁹): the burn-mode scan-channel ratchet
(4447), the term that currently breaks the plane in play time. Expected gain: the fence
turns an unbounded, K-growing slope (~12.7 MB per held scan at 10⁵) into a flat line
measured to 42k game-s; what it deletes is the answer-or-expire fence's open half — the
record had priced the ratchet (4447) and flown the oldest scope (1b3f), but never flown
expiry or measured its add-back.

## The five lessons (the lens's deliverable)

1. **Delete the retention, budget the add-back.** *"Delete any part or process you can.
   You may have to add them back later. In fact, if you do not end up adding back at
   least 10% of them, then you didn't delete enough."* — Elon Musk, Walter Isaacson.
   Mechanism: pending work's *retention* is a part like any other; the add-back rate is
   the honesty check that keeps deletion from being procrastinated re-work. Change: a
   held scan older than 16 game-s is dropped unanswered (`ES_SCAN_TTL_S`, off = shipped
   bit for bit); the add-back = encounters the drop loses. Flown here: 227+263 drops
   lost ≤ 10 encounters (< 0.3%, bar met with 30× margin). Cheapest test: this run's
   arm B (`regen.sh`).
2. **Ruin, not smoothness: bound the worst case, in bytes, at the target.** *"The one
   thing you have to avoid is the risk of ruin."* — Naval Ravikant (Almanack). The
   ratchet is ruin (4 GiB in one wall-hour at 10⁴–10⁵, 4447), not a cost; no average
   justifies it. Change: `SCAN_BACKLOG`'s count cap (2048) is dimensionally wrong —
   2048 scans × 12.7 MB = 26 GB at 10⁵, and it never engaged before the guard in any
   flight; the fence must count pinned **bytes** (b77d-1fcf's clause) and be sized by
   arithmetic at 10⁶ (TTL·λ·(K+38)·224 B ≤ budget), never by an observed rate. Cheapest
   test: the 10⁶ arithmetic on the measured constants (this dossier's numbers).
3. **The queue is not the arrivals; service is.** *"The waiting room is always full."*
   (a mentor's line, reported by Ali) — Sahil Bloom's Curiosity Chronicle. Mechanism:
   arrivals are structural (the wildcard's pacing, 2403: the player's own clock mints);
   bounding the queue does not slow the mint — service must keep up or the fence pays
   the difference in dropped answers. Change: the fence is the backstop; the cure stays
   2403's margin (0 held at a 24 ms budget with identical arrivals). Cheapest test: one
   TTL arm at 24 ms — predicted: expiry never fires.
4. **Subtract the mint itself where the scan cannot hit.** *"What if I could only
   subtract to solve problems?"* — Tim Ferriss (Tools of Titans). Mechanism: the
   interstellar player's capture still walks all 100,028 bodies (`radial`'s no-doll
   fallback) and pins a 22-MB candidates Vec per mint — the scan that cannot hit is the
   part to delete (7f99's "promotion = refusal filter", here at the mint). Change: an
   admission filter — no doll, no candidates, no scan. Cheapest test: a print-only
   count of mints with zero in-doll candidates on this arm (predicted: the majority
   past departure).
5. **Fix the wall and it moves; re-run the ladder, don't tune the dead one.** *"The
   Theory of Constraints … a system will never grow beyond its biggest constraint or
   bottleneck."* — Anthony Vicino. With the ratchet deleted, this flight's next N-growing
   terms are the view gather (2.1 s warm at 10⁵ here, dcf1's law) and the frame channel
   (p99 11.9 ms both arms). Change: after the fence lands, the lab's next nladder run is
   the full-ladder re-measure, not another scan-channel iteration. Cheapest test: one
   `ladder_digest`-class flight per rung 10⁴–10⁶ post-fence.
