# Red-team of B12 v2 before freeze (20261012-1715-redteam-b12-v2-5dcb)

**Claim.** The B12 v2 statistic is broken as a fleet gate: it returns PASS (`handoff::b12`, official detail line) for a law that leaks at the gate's own declared detectable rate — 2e-10 relative Jacobi per horizon — on 5 of the 16 corpus ships, because the pooled rank score is a one-sided mean over ships that any bounded majority error cancels; and its rank null is miscalibrated by many orders of magnitude.

**Bottleneck.** The `b12-statistic` topic (the integrator's pre-freeze queue: "B12 is being redesigned from the requirement, then red-teamed again"). Not a board engine bottleneck; this decides whether the candidate may freeze.

The gate's own dt-coefficient fits the leak exactly on the leaking ships (leak column −2.00e-10/horizon, all five) and the verdict ignores it — the deciding statistic is the pooled rank score, not the fit it prints.
2. **The mechanism — cancellation in a one-sided pool.** A bounded blip alone (amplitude 1e-6, zero at both horizon ends) scores pooled **−2.89** (w = 0.02) / **−5.19** (w = 0.05) / **−13.64** (w = 0.1), all `pass=false` with sensitivity UNSUPPORTED: `arrow <= ARROW_MAX` would accept the negative ones without comment (the author's sharper blip scored −32.7/−34.4, a ~1e-230 event under the candidate's rank null). The same magnitude with positive sign fails (+28.0 at the 8/8 hump split); the test has no lower bound and no per-ship decision.
3. **False positive on the affine assumption.** `quad(k)` — a memoryless error k·((a−a₀)/R_H)²·|C₀|, a function of the single state it is laid on, like the passing `planted-bounded` but quadratic — FAILs at every amplitude (arrow +9.579/+9.598/+9.665; worst err 8.9e-8 at k = 4e-11 up to 8.9e-6 at k = 4e-9; z scale-free: rank verdicts do not depend on amplitude), with sensitivity RESOLVED, i.e. confident "compounding". Its linear cousin at a larger error (worst 1.28e-5) PASSES. The PASS region is a model class (affine in the slow state), not an error size or type.
4. **Calibration where it decides.** The creep scan (linear leak λ on echo's histories) is a cliff at the 1e-12 reporting floor, not at the 5 cutoff: λ ≤ 1e-13 keeps every ship below FLOOR (arrow exactly 0.000, PASS), λ = 1e-12 gives 39.1 (FAIL). No λ lands near 5; the cutoff decides nothing on clean laws. **Predictions** (`predictions.md`, written before the decisive runs): quad FAIL at every k — held (arrow +9.6 as predicted, ≥ +15 was too strong); hump z sign uncertain in (−25, +10) — landed +1.9, in range; slab τ = 5 predicted PASS-side or UNSUPPORTED — **wrong** (it fails at +22.7: I underestimated that pairs are always first-half×second-half, so any early transient ranks as exposure); half predicted FAIL or a narrow window — the even 8/8 split fails (+9.9 to +23.9 across blip widths, rebuilt shapes), the 5/11 minority split found the window (that variant post-dates the prediction, reported as such).

The break is in the verdict logic, not the physics layer: the corpus, the injection, the pairing and the leak fit all worked (the fit names the leak to 3 digits on every leaking ship). Cheapest fixes, in order: (a) decide per ship — no ship's z may exceed a calibrated cutoff (the per-ship null is also broken: planted-bounded's ship 0 sits at +5.8 under it, so the cutoff needs calibration, e.g. by permuting dt labels within each ship's pairs); (b) bound the printed leak fit per ship (|leak| ≤ DETECTABLE_LEAK) instead of discarding it; (c) keep the pool only as a diagnostic. Cost: one extra pass per law over data the gate already computes; no new corpus, no new physics. Two-sided |arrow| alone would fail more bounded laws (the blip is bounded), so it is not a …

Fit with PRINCIPLES: the pooled mean contradicts "behaviours matter, not continuum fidelity" (P2) and "the simulation tells us what's next" (P22) — a player's ship drifts or it does not; a fleet average answers a different question. The affine assumption contradicts P19 ("a fix signals the wrong representation"): the statistic needed a patch (the state gradient) and the patch has a hole (quad). "A test that cannot fail is a control" cuts both ways: this test cannot *pass* an entire family of bounded laws, so as written it is a size gate wearing a type gate's clothes.

**The lens (what red-team may have missed).** I did not attack the corpus selection itself (16 ships, public, frozen — my a₀/sep > 1.13 split overfits it; a real law's regime boundary lands wherever it lands, and any boundary that leaves ≥ ~2/3 of the corpus on the clean side at λ ≤ 2e-10 passes), nor the surface/fate domain (B13's oracle), nor the plane's UNSUPPORTED verdict on Linux. The cancellation needs the majority error to be shaped (blip); a law with a *uniform* bounded majority error leaks visibly (+28.0 at 8/8). The gate is not trivially fooled — it is fooled by exactly the shape a regime boundary produces.
