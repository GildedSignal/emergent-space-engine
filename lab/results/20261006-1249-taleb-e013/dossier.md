# Dossier — taleb-e013: the phase-neighbour train's k is not a constant; it grows with fleet size, and at the 10⁶ scale it was adopted for it silently misses most real encounters

## Claim (one sentence)
The gold's phase-neighbour certificate (`20261006-0346-taleb-09b5`: "every measured encounter sits within distance-rank 4 … a phase-neighbor train certificate at O(n_c·k) replaces the quadratic tier") is an artifact of n_c ≤ 800 with random perihelion spread: the required k scales as k ≈ 1 + 2·d̄_min·d_λ (true-longitude line density), measured linear in n_c (μ̂/n = 7.0–7.5×10⁻⁶), so at the 10⁶ fleet that motivated it the k=4 train silently misses ≈94% of real encounters (3.4% missed already at n=128,000) and the honest k ≈ 16 restores ~1.1 GB of store — and at n=800, a formation with correlated perihelion (ϖ aligned, how fleets fly) breaks even the gold's distance-rank ≤ 4 observation (max 23, 29% of encounters beyond rank 4).

## Bottleneck
The board's top scaling risk (candidate work must track real encounters). The gold replaced the quadratic tier with the train; this run maps the train's own validity boundary. The invariant at stake: a sub-ρ contact needs |Δλ| ≤ d_min < ρ, so the bodies with true longitude inside the contact window — λ-adjacent but radially far (radial spread a·e ≈ 14ρ decorrelates their 3D distance) — sit between the two partners in the sorted order and consume watch slots. Longitude-order equals distance-order only while ρ·d_λ ≪ 1. The gold's ϖ ~ U(−0.1, 0.1) randomised true longitude (spread 0.058 rad ≫ any bunching), which is exactly why rank stayed ≤ 4 in its runs — the certificate was validated on the workload that cannot break it.
