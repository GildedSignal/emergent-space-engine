# Dossier — 20261011-0324-fermi-c211 (Fermi lens)

**Claim.** At 10⁹ bodies on one M1, kick compute on short orbits is the only budget term that
breaks (1.3×–2,400× over in the plausible corners; the board's "about 190 cores" re-derives to
0.5% as the all-billion-on-81-minute-orbits corner), memory is an admission rule — bits/body ≈
1 + 61·f_cum against a 4 GiB budget, so it breaks only past a cumulative kicked fraction
f_cum ≈ 0.55, with program-free bodies capped at 2.3×10⁸ — and H_live is bounded for thousands
of hours because one kick creates only ~23 bits (61-bit scar, 110-ns seal; all three measured).

**Bottleneck.** Board item "fitting 10⁹ in 8 GB … decided by distinctions created per
game-second" and bottleneck 3 ("cost at a billion … about 190 cores"). Gain: the 10⁹ question
reduces from "bytes × count" to two measured knobs — **orbit throughput** (body-orbit
completions/s, the scarce compute resource) and **cumulative kicked fraction** (the memory
admission rule). What it deletes: any per-body memory scaling work below f_cum ≈ 0.5 (34×
headroom at f=0), any bandwidth work (0.02 GB/s at capacity events, random access is
latency-bound), and the fear that H_live rates threaten 8 GB (4 GiB fills in ≈ 5,500 h of
RTF-100 galactic play even with sealing disabled).

regenerated by `sh reproduce.sh`, each step < 60 s; raw JSONL in `data/`):

*The admission curve* (ladder, store keeps the smaller of program+scars / packed):
bits/body = 1.0 (f=0) → 1.65 (1%) → 6.9 (10%) → 18.1 (30%) → 53.5 (100%, packed wins); program
re-inference holds through f = 10% (it fails at 30%), while the program+scar form still wins to
f between 0.3 and 1. So **bits/body ≈ 1 + 61·f_cum** (measure confirms:
+5.9 to +9.2 bits after kicking 10% of members once). 4 GiB at 10⁹ = 34.4 bits/body →
**f_cum ≤ 0.55**; 10% disturbance costs 868 MB (4.9× headroom). A program-free population
(traders: 149.7 bits/body, within 3% of its 145.5 entropy floor) caps at **2.3×10⁸ bodies**.

*The compute envelope* (`python3 budget.py`, full table in `data/budget-output.txt`):
M1 capacity = 8 cores ÷ 29 µs/kick (plane, H0210) = 2.8×10⁵ kicks/s = **8.6×10³ body-orbit/s
at 32 kicks/orbit, 9.2×10⁵ at the 0.3 averaged cadence (H0190)**. Demand = N/T·RTF:

| scene (all 10⁹) | RTF 1, 32k | RTF 100, 32k | RTF 100, 0.3k |
|---|---|---|---|
| year orbits | 272× under | 2.7× under | 290× under |
| day orbits | 1.3× over | 134× over | 1.3× over |
| 81-min orbits | **24× over (= 191 cores)** | 2,387× over | 22× over |

The board's "about 190 cores" is exactly the 81-minute-orbit corner (23.9× × 8 = 191): a scene
statement, not a wall. Encounter activity at the r8 v2 rate (0.012/body/orbit, year orbits):
38 encounters/s at RTF 100 → 0.22% of one core in kicks, ~10⁻⁶ in seals, H_live creation
1.7×10³ bit/s. Re-fit (full-population stream pass): 1.4 s, i.e. 1.4% of one core at one pass
per 100 s. **Verdict order: compute (short-orbit corner) ≫ memory (f_cum > 0.55) > H_live rate
(never, at these rates) > bandwidth (never; latency-bound).**

**Predictions** (`predictions.md`, written before all runs): the structure held — compute binds
on short orbits, memory is admission-controlled, bandwidth never binds, the averaging cadence is
mandatory at RTF 100 for a billion (2.7× headroom unaveraged on year orbits). Calibration
misses, reported: traders/stream are far less compressible than I guessed (149.7 / 25.3 vs
≤0.5 / ≤5 predicted — the gold's 0.126-bit belt is the ladder's f=0 rung, not a wide-range
population); the hot path is 3–30× *better* than predicted (28–31 ns vs 100–1000); compaction is
10–100× *worse* than the hot path (0.9–8 µs/update) and does **not** reabsorb kicked members
(+5.9…+9.2 bits vs ≤1 predicted — microstates are canonical; only the ladder's seal path
returns the 60 bits); battle kicks add almost nothing (+0.7 bits) because that population
already sits at its entropy floor (164.8 of 170.9).

**What would make it wrong.** (1) The 29 µs/kick was measured at the plane's ~10³–10⁴ bodies;
at 10⁹ the doll-tree walk and cache misses per kick may grow it — if the real cost is 10×
(≈300 µs), every compute row shifts one decade worse and year-orbit-unaveraged-RTF-100 flips
to 2.7× over. (2) The encounter rate is r8's ρ ∈ [10⁻⁴,10⁻³] belt, not a game scene; a 10×
denser belt still only costs 2.2% of a core, but co-orbital fleets (taleb-09b5) break the
*discovery* tier, which this budget imports as constant work per encounter. (3) The ladder's
kick model is a small delta (±2¹⁰ on 48-bit fields); the measure/compaction path at larger
deltas is untested. (4) x86 timings; the integrator re-measures on the M1.

**Cost of adoption:** none in code; `budget.py` + the two constants (61 bits/scar, 23 bits/kick)
feed the belt design (B18b) and the queued `b-h-live` run; the admission rule (f_cum ≤ 0.55,
free-body ceiling 2.3×10⁸) belongs in the plane's population seam as a budget check.

**Fit with PRINCIPLES.** "The memory floor is information" (#24): confirmed by measurement —
the store sits within 3% of the floor for hard populations and at 1 bit/body overhead for easy
ones, and the floor, not bytes×count, is what the admission rule enforces. "No work for no
reason" (#14): the seal/re-fit pricing (110 ns, 1.4 s/pass) is pay-per-distinction. "Each scale
resolves only what matters at that scale" (#33): the two knobs are exactly the scale-separation.

**The lens.** Fermi reduced everything to two knobs and may have missed what they hide: the
view gather (83% of a light frame, H0210) is player-bounded and excluded; the doll *tree's* own
memory at 10⁹ is unmeasured; the GPU path is uncosted; and the scar lifetime between seals —
the p99 hitch from a compaction burst (max_step 2.7 µs measured, arena up to 8 µs) — is a
smoothness question (bottleneck 4), not a capacity one, and this lens did not measure its tail.
