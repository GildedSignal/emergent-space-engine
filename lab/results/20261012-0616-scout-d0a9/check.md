Grade: silver

**Abstract.** Claim: live-scar memory follows a first-passage law, t_sat ≈ (Q/σ_s)² orbits, ~1 bit/scar, so a real 10⁹ belt never scars (fits as ~16 MB of programs). Everything reproduces bit-identically from the shipped scripts (≤ 30 s each); all quoted numbers verified; predictions pre-registered before the decisive run. But the central mechanism claim — "the rate decays (range law ~√k), so memory self-saturates even with no recompaction" — is falsified by a 13 s probe: the scar stream is linear at √(2/π)·σ_s/Q per orbit per body, forever (0.1994/0.0997/0.0499 at qfac 4/8/16 over 40 000 orbits). The belt headline survives (qfac ~10⁵ at view-floor: nothing flows); recompaction is mandatory for anything run past t_sat, and analyze.py ships an axis bug. Cost to adopt: a corrected design note plus the b-h-live instruments.

**Reasons.**

1. **Reproduction: exact.** `hlive.py results-check.json` and `analyze.py` regenerate `results.json` and `curves.json` bit-for-bit (29.9 s each); `fermi.py` matches its table (real belt t_relax 2.92e22 orbits, t_sat 2.9e10/2.9e4 orbits — arithmetic independently verified). Kick rms A/D = 1.277/1.670e-3 and 1.298/1.690e-3, max/rms 4.9–5.5, lag-1 ≤ 0.028; payload bits 1.00–1.04 / 1.94 / 2.94 / 4.74 at qfac ≥ 3 / 1 / 0.5 / 0.15; saturation 0.992–1.000 / 0.750–0.910 / 0.215–0.457 / 0.004–0.078 at qfac 4/8/16/32; control max |ΔA| = 1.22e-15/orbit, zero scars — all as stated.
2. **Timing.** predictions.md (06:29:26) precedes results.json (06:37:36) and curves.json (06:38:06); its calibration cites a separate 10-orbit run. Scripts are deterministic, so the post-dossier mtimes are benign re-runs (outputs identical).
3. **The law that is right.** First-passage saturation verified where the 40-orbit window reaches (measured k50 = 5–6 at qfac 4, 15–21 at qfac 8, matching the correct MC's 5 and 15), and extended by correct MC to qfac 64: k50/qfac² → 0.20. Payload ≈ 1 bit at qfac ≥ 5. Control exact. The view-floor belt conclusion (qfac ~10⁵–10⁶ ⇒ nothing scars in any epoch) is robust under both the author's and the correct stream law.
4. **The mechanism claim that is wrong (my probe A, `/tmp/check-d0a9/probe_longrun.py`).** The dossier diagnoses P1's failure as "a free walk's scar count grows as the range law ~√k, so the rate decays and the memory self-saturates even with no recompaction". A Gaussian walk run to k = 40 000 (N = 4096, qfac 4/8/16) gives scars/body = 7978.0/3989.2/1994.3 — linear in k at 0.1994/0.0997/0.0499 per orbit = √(2/π)·σ/Q to three significant figures (the sigma-delta carry is a renewal process: each quantum of accumulated total variation crosses a cell). The √k range law counts *distinct cells*, not crossings; a scar log is crossings. So the naive identity σ²/Q² does not over-count — it *under*-counts the post-saturation stream by ~qfac/5, and the author's own sweep already sits on 0.8/qfac (rate·qfac² = 3.5–5 at qfac 5–8, i.e. ~3 scars/body/40 orbits at qfac 8, not the 0.6 the identity predicts). Consequence: "self-saturates even with no recompaction" is false; any population run past ~t_sat streams at 0.8·σ_s/Q per orbit per body, so recompaction (or storing the current offset register instead of an append-only log) is load-bearing, not optional. The km-scale-quantum row (t_sat = 2.9e4 orbits, "beyond any game epoch") is the fragile one: by 10⁴ orbits first passages have begun and the stream then flows at ~5e-3/orbit.
5. **Shipped-artifact bug (my finding).** `analyze.py` line 32: `cum_mc.mean(axis=0)` averages over orbits, not bodies; the stored `gauss_mc_cum` "curves" are 256 per-body fractions (measured_cum is 40 points). The `mc_end` saturation table is computed on a separate correct line and is right. But (a) the dossier's "tracks the whole curve within ±0.13 absolute" is not regenerable from the shipped code — the correct whole-curve bound is ±0.172 (seed1 D qfac8); (b) the dossier's P7 numbers "0.76–0.83× the Gaussian reference at k=10" were computed against the broken curve; correct ratios are 0.84–0.96. P7's verdict (failed in direction) is unchanged.

Scars as a store come from shipmem (H ~3088, "program plus scars") and 0.126 bits/body from components-98bc; the first-passage law, the payload entropy and t_sat are new. **The one experiment for gold.** Fold the long-run probe back in: replace the range-law diagnosis with the total-variation stream law (rate = √(2/π)·σ_s/Q per orbit per body after t_sat), fix `analyze.py`'s axis, restate the km-quantum row and the recompaction requirement from it, and re-verify the whole-curve MC bound (±0.17, not ±0.13). That is a ≤ 60 s rerun plus a dossier edit; the design note is then adoptable as-is.

**What the author missed.**
- The renewal/total-variation structure of their own sigma-delta: the answer (rate ≈ 0.8·σ/Q at qfac 5–8) was in their sweep table, and they read it as a decaying rate.
- The axis bug in the artifact they quoted two numbers from (whole-curve bound, P7 ratios) — a check that "the shipped code regenerates the stated numbers" would have caught both.
- Minor overstatements: "rate·qfac² = 2–8 at qfac 8–32" is actually 0.10–8.05 (the 0.10/0.60 entries are 1–2 scars total — noise); "1.00–1.04 at Q ≥ 5σ" is 1.00–1.01 at 5σ (1.03–1.04 is at 3σ); P4 is counted "held" although qfac 0.15 gives 4.74 bits, outside its own predicted [2, 4].
- The offset register width (current cell + carry) grows only as ½·log₂ k — an alternative to recompaction the dossier never considers, and the cheaper half of the fix.
