# Dossier — 20261006-1135-quant-a4ed (quant lens)

**Claim.** Measured on the lab box, the billion-body event budget splits into three walls — kick compute (today 29 µs: even 0.3 kicks/orbit leaves a 10⁹-body battle ~2× short of 10× real time), kick layout (independent ≤4-line gathers sustain 2.7× a pointer-chasing path), and cadence (64→0.3 kicks/orbit = 213×) — and no layout reaches a 10⁹-body battle at 10× real time on today's 64-kick cadence, while year-scale belts already pass 10⁹ at 10× real time even at today's 29 µs per kick.

**Bottleneck.** Board "Cost at a billion" (10⁹ ≈ 190 cores today, want 10–100× less) and focus item 1 (fewer kicks per orbit). This run does not speed anything up; it prices the levers separately, which the board currently blends into one "10–100×". The model reproduces the board's independent 190-core estimate within 1.8× (needs 1.19e7 ev/s for a 10⁹ battle at 1× RTF; today's kick gives 2.76e5/box ⇒ ~344 vCPU ⇒ ~190 M1-class cores), which corroborates the method.

**Evidence** (all in `work/`, each command ≤ 60 s; box: i7-4790 4C/8T DDR3, load 0.3–2.7, package 61 °C; orders of magnitude only — the integrator re-measures on the M1, whose 68 GB/s and lack of SMT shift the stream and chase numbers in opposite directions, stated below).

- `bench.c` measures events/s when each event touches L cold 64-B lines under three layouts: **chase** (dependent chain, latency-bound), **gather** (L independent chains, MLP-bound — the realistic case once the event list is known before loads issue), **stream** (prefetched). Rebuild: `gcc -O2 -march=native -o bench bench.c -lpthread`; run `./bench <regime> <L> <threads> [512]`.
- Matrix (48 cells, `raw.jsonl`): single-core ns/line: chase 81–99 ns at every L (DRAM latency, flat in L); gather 25.2 ns/line at L=4, 17.9 at L=8; stream 4.5. All-core (8T): chase-L4 1.74e7 ev/s, gather-L4 4.74e7 (2.7×), gather-L8 2.56e7, stream capped at the box's 21.8 GB/s ⇒ 8.5e7 at L=4 (4.9× chase). Stability: key cell re-run under load 2.3: 44.1e6 (−7%).
- Controls, both sides: cache-resident (1 MiB) chase-L4 is 8.2× its DRAM rate (confirms memory-boundness); stream single-core = 13–15 GB/s and aggregate 21.8 GB/s, i.e. pinned to DDR3 hardware limits (confirms the counting is honest).
- `budget.py` → `budget.json`: N_max = rate × P / (kicks-per-orbit × RTF), scenes = battle (P = 90 min) and belt (P = 1 yr), cadence ∈ {64, 0.3}/orbit (step.rs; H0190), RTF ∈ {1, 10}. Verdict table in the output; headline: battle/64/RTF10 fails for every layout (best 4.0e8 = gather-4); battle/0.3/RTF10 passes for every memory layout with ≥30× margin but still fails with today's 29 µs compute (4.97e8 < 1e9; passes once a kick costs < ~15 µs); belt/64/RTF10 passes even at 29 µs/kick (1.4e10).

**Predictions** (`predictions.md`, written before the decisive matrix; smoke cells declared there): 6 of 8 held — gather/chase 4-thread scaling ≥3.5× (✓ 3.7×/3.8×), gather-8T ≤1.3× of 4T (✓ 1.29×), gather L=8 ns/line ≤ L=4 and ev/s ≈ half (✓ 4.88≤5.28, 0.54×), belts pass everywhere (✓), cadence multiplier exactly 64/0.3 (✓), gather fails 10⁹-battles at 10× RTF (✓). Failed: (4) resident chase ≥20× DRAM — measured 8.2×; cause identified (L2 latency ~12 ns is itself the resident floor; I assumed ~2 ns); (6) chase caps *below* 1e9 at battle/1× — measured 1.47e9, i.e. it sits at the goal with no margin, one order honest but the sign of the clause was wrong. Prediction 5 (perf LLC-miss count) was blocked by the host (`perf_event_paranoid=4`), not by the data; the bandwidth-pin and resident controls stand in.

**What would make it wrong.** x86→M1 transfer (no SMT: the latency-bound chase loses its 1.8× hyperthread lift, so the gather/stream advantage is a floor; 68 GB/s helps stream ~3×; both shifts favour the conclusion, but the absolute N_max cells must be re-measured); L=4 cold lines/kick is an assumption (grounded in step.rs: record + queue + moments — but not counted on the real path); the model charges only kicks — encounters, contacts during a battle, and the view gather (83% of a light frame today) are extra events I did not price; one working-set size (512 MiB) — larger footprints add TLB pressure. Adversarial cases worth running next: a burst (a player action waking many cells) priced at p99.9, and the real kick loop's cold-line count on the plane.

**Cost of adopting.** None in code: a rule and a table. The rule for every kick-path change (motion2, wt/kick, the allocator): (a) count cold lines per kick, (b) they must be independent, ≤4, (c) kick compute must sit under the ~15 µs battle-scale budget — below that, memory decides, and gather-not-chase is worth 2.7×. Fit with PRINCIPLES: "we need to be deeply aware of von-neumann bottleneck and memory" (CL 09-27 09:08); "no work for no reason" (CL 09-29) — cadence is exactly that lever, and it multiplies with layout, never substitutes for it; "i care about the p95/99 results" (CL 09-25) — the budget is stated per scene, not on the average body.

**The lens.** Quant measured capacity, not tail latency: the p99.9/worst-frame question (a burst's wake at c, heat on a fanless M1) is untouched and is this lens's own headline — a next run should price one player action as a market order. It also missed the GPU entirely (the M1's dense order could move the gather row). And a pure microbench shares no harness with the engine, but also proves nothing about the engine's real loop: the cold-line count on the real kick path is the missing measurement, and it needs one instrumented run of the plane.
