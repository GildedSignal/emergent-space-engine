**What the author missed.**

- **A number the stored log cannot regenerate:** the dossier's resident control "8.2× its DRAM rate" — the author's own `raw.jsonl` gives 27.21M/2.54M = 10.7× (my rerun: 8.8×). It looks like a re-run quoted without its log. It drives no verdict (any value ≥8 confirms memory-boundness and prediction 4 fails either way), but it is exactly the slip the lab rules name; keep the provenance clean.
- **The stream control is single-sampled and fragile at 512 MiB:** my repeats gave 414–580M ev/s (26–37 GB/s, above DDR3's ceiling — inter-thread L3 hits between the 8 sweepers); only at 1024 MiB does it settle at 344M = 22.0 GB/s. The 21.8 GB/s cap used in the budget happens to be right, but the "counting is honest" confirmation should have used the larger footprint.
- **"Both shifts favour the conclusion" is only half true for the M1.** No-SMT favours the gather/chase ratio, but 68 GB/s moves the ALL-fail battle cell the other way: a perfectly streamed 4-line kick at 68 GB/s bounds N_max at 2.24e9 > 1e9. The verdict that survives transfer is the compute wall (29 µs fails even at 0.3/orbit on any 8-core box) and the cadence lever; the "every layout fails" cell is DDR3-specific and should be re-measured, as the author does say.
- The 190-core "corroboration" reuses the same 29 µs constant the board used, so it checks the arithmetic, not the physics; and the 29 µs row assumes the plane's kick cost parallelises linearly over 8 vCPU, machine unstated.
