# Dossier — the plane's cliff under load: no wall hitch, silent zero-RTF stalls, and a digest that holds

Run: 20261008-1252-taleb-2570 (lens Taleb), Linux x86 runner, 8 vCPU, load 1.3–2.0, ~15 GB RAM.
All numbers are the shipped bench's own JSON (`xtask universe --bench`), regenerated by the
commands below, each ≤ 60 s. Machine state is recorded inside every JSON (`machine_before/after`,
uptime). Orders of magnitude only (cloud x86, not the M1).

## Claim

At 100× RTF the plane's "no hitch" survives 32× load because the frame's wall cost saturates
(~14–17 ms p99/worst, ≤ 1 frame over 16.7 ms), but the failure moves into **silent lag**: the
worst 1% of frames present **zero** new game time from 4× members on (rtf.lowest_1pct: 9.9× at 48
members → 0.00 at 192), with no refusal and no lost state — and under that overload the canonical
digest stays bit-identical across engine budgets 0.5/5/50 ms, threads 1/4, paced/unpaced, so the
budget provably moves work and never decisions at the cliff.

## Bottleneck

Board bottleneck 4 (smoothness and the allocator; Julien: "i care about the p95/99 results", "the
algorithm that allocates the work to prevent those heavy tails, it's the most important one").
Expected gain: not a speedup — it retargets the allocator from wall hitches (which clamp) to
content stalls (which do not), and it flags the view gather (64–69% of the frame, unclamped, max
~10 ms) as the remaining unbounded synchronous claim that plane.md §7 forbids. It also deletes
trust in two dashboard readings: `stats` counters (schedule-dependent) and the `--members` knob
(saturates near 768).

## Predictions

`predictions.md` was written before the ladder. What happened: P3 (digest invariance at the
cliff) HELD — the load-bearing one. P2 held in shape (silent lag, no refusal) but the cliff sits
at the baseline on this box, not at 192–768. P1 was half wrong: the wall tail saturates rather
than growing convexly — the clamping works; my "gather grows linearly" model was wrong (it grows
sublinearly but stays dominant and unclamped). P4 held (refused 0 everywhere) with the caveat
that `lost` is schedule-dependent, not a death toll. P5 held (bytes/body 4.8–6.3 KB; no memory
cliff at this scale). Negative controls behaved (m1536 and rtf1000 both show overflow/dip).

## What would make it wrong

On the M1 (≈4× faster per H0211's p99 2.7 vs 10.7 ms) the zero-stall regime may start near 16×
members instead of 4×; the shape (saturating wall, stall-to-zero tail, unclamped gather) is what
transfers, the locations are this machine's. The zero-RTF reading depends on the dashboard's
per-frame `game_ticks` being the presented horizon's move (dash.rs `record`); a checker should
confirm with the http view that a stalled frame really re-presents the same tick. The m1536≡m768
digest equality is evidence, not proof, of generator saturation; prove it by counting bodies at
genesis (`universe --describe` at 1536 members lists them).

## Cost of adoption, and fit

Nothing to merge: this is measurement on main's shipped bench (plus this dossier). The costs are
attention: (1) the smoothness service level to gate on is `rtf.lowest_1pct`, already
instrumented, with a kill criterion like "no frame presents zero ticks at 4× the design load";
(2) the gather must be budgeted or clipped before any allocator work claims the frame; (3) fix
or cap the bench's members knob before trusting rungs above ~768. Fit — PRINCIPLES: "Smoothness;
the allocator … 'i care about the p95/99 results i want the game to be smooth'"; "Errors never
compound" (the verified digest invariance is that principle holding under stress); "a test that
cannot fail is a control" (both controls here fired).
