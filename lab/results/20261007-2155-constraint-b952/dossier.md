# Run 20261007-2155-constraint-b952 — the constraint lens

**Claim.** On the plane as the player runs it, the frame's constraint is not the engine (kicks) but the observation walk: `view_gather` re-reads every visible emitter every frame at full precision — 65% of summed frame CPU, ~10× the engine's median, unbudgeted and unamortized — and it breaks the 60 Hz frame at ~10³ visible emitters (4–18 µs each), 3–6 orders below the goal's sky; at the instrument's own resolution only ~8% of emitters change per frame (rtf 100), so a surprise-gated view (validity interval per emitter, bounded by the crossing's arrival rate — already computed for the Newton bracket) is the exploit, worth ~12× on this scene and unbounded on the far sky (measured 0% change at 10⁸ ticks).

**Bottleneck.** Board focus line 1 is "kicks dominate the engine's cost" — true *inside* the engine's own 5 ms budget (its p99 is pinned at 5.1 ms by design, work deferred). But the frame as flown spends its median 0.89 ms in the view vs 0.09 ms in the engine. The engine is surprise-driven and budget-deferrable; the view is the frame's only unconditional O(N_visible) term. Whatever happens to kicks, the view alone caps the game at ~10³ resolved emitters at 60 Hz. Expected gain of the gate: ≥ 10× fewer readouts per frame at today's scenes; it deletes the "walk the sky every frame" component that would otherwise need GPU elevation (the expensive answer).

**Evidence** (all Linux/x86 shared runner, 8 vCPU, load 1.2–1.8, orders of magnitude only; commands ≤ 60 s):

1. The frame, as the player runs it (`cargo run --release -p xtask -- universe --bench --rtf 100 --seconds 30`, solo):
   `view_gather` p50 **0.894 ms**, p99 4.83 ms, **share 65%**; `engine` p50 **0.093 ms**, p99 5.11 ms (at its 5 ms budget), share 21%; view_json p50 0.45 ms; wire 0.19 ms; frame_cpu p50 1.35 ms / p99 10.6 ms. (An earlier parallel-loaded run inflated view_gather p50 to 3.6 ms; the solo CPU-time numbers are the honest ones. Machine state recorded in `view_gate.out`.)
2. Scaling, isolated (`cargo run --release -p emergent-universe --example view_scale 60`, the `stream` scene, memo cache, rtf 100): per-emitter per-frame cost is **linear** 24 → 2400 emitters: 4.1 µs slow (exactly 1.00 reads/emitter), 13.3 µs at 0.9c toward (1.6 reads). At 2400 emitters the gather alone is 9.9 ms — 59% of a 16.7 ms frame.
3. The unit cost on the plane's own scene (same example, part B, aged 600 frames): **10.7 µs per readout** averaged (1.01 reads/emitter/frame — the Newton memo is already perfect); raw tube+chain without the ramp: 7.2 µs. Fresh-scene heliocentric ships: 18.4 µs/call (planets 3.4, moon-depth 6.7–8.9). So the tube readout itself, not the doll-chain walk and not the ramp (~30%), is the unit.
4. Surprise fraction at instrument resolution (`cargo run --release -p emergent-universe --example view_gate 400 100`, quantum 10⁻³ rad ≈ one pixel at 60° FOV/1080p, or |Δshift| 0.01): plane at rtf 100 **8.1%** of emitter-frames move one quantum (mean Δθ 3.6×10⁻⁴, max 6.4×10⁻³); 0.9c-toward stream at 10⁶/10⁷ ticks 7.7–8.6% (the movers sweep ~0.1 rad — exactly the near/fast content); at 10⁸ ticks **0.0%** (max 5×10⁻⁵: the far sky is frozen frame-to-frame). Counter-case: fresh battle at rtf 1, 49.7% move — the gate's worst case is a close, fresh scene, where it still halves the walk.
5. Gate state on record (HANDOFF 6 October, not re-run here): B13 60/64, B17 pass, E1–E8 pass, E9 fails phase 1 as designed, B12 miscalibrated — no gate failure names the view; this is a blind spot of the board's focus list, not of a gate.

**Predictions** (`predictions.md`, written before the decisive run): (2) held — 8.1% < 30% at rtf 100, median well under 10⁻⁴; (3) held in structure — amortization follows distance (0% at 10⁸ ticks), not body count; the near movers at 10⁶–10⁷ ticks are ~8% because they are few, not because they are slow. (1) **failed**: the ramp is only ~30% of the readout (10.7 vs 7.2 µs), not >70% — the tube readout itself is the bulk, so the *next* constraint after the gate is the readout unit cost (7–18 µs for a compiled-orbit read; a representation question: the compiled generator should make this a few adds).

**What would make it wrong.** (a) The instrument resolving finer than 10⁻³ rad: zoom raises the angular demand ("the zoom level is the minimap"); the quantum must track zoom, and under deep zoom the gate's saving shrinks toward the worst case. (b) Observer manoeuvres: aberration from the *player's* changing velocity shifts every emitter's apparent direction together — but that is one transform over cached root-frame directions (O(N) at ~4 floats each), not a re-read; the gate must live on root-frame quantities, with aberration applied as a per-frame overlay. (c) If contacts/radar must be exact every frame at fine distance quanta, the distance observable gates too (not measured here; direction and shift were). (d) The wire: at 10³–10⁴ emitters json+wire (~2.9 µs/emitter) becomes a co-constraint; the changed-only view must also serialize changed-only.

**Cost of adopting.** Small and local: `ViewCache` grows a per-emitter validity interval (bounded by the arrival rate it already stores for the Newton bracket and by the observables' rate bounds); `gather_with` iterates expired emitters instead of all; the page serializes the delta. The view becomes approximate *at the instrument's resolution by construction* — staleness is bounded every frame and cannot compound (PRINCIPLES #13, error type not size). Judged by behaviour gates, not view equality. No engine change.

**Fit with PRINCIPLES.** #3 "Nothing is evolved that can be derived when looked at (clocks, redshift, views)" — the walk re-derives every crossing every frame at full precision the instrument cannot resolve; #14 "no work for no reason"; #33 "a body resolves only the influences that are significant at its own scale" — here, the *observer* resolves only changes significant at its own resolution; #28 "The view is an instrument". The gate is the same surprise principle the engine already lives by, applied to observation.

**The lens.** Goldratt's constraint analysis can itself miss a constraint outside the measured subsystem: I looked where the board's focus list pointed (kicks) and found the frame's median elsewhere. What this lens may have made me miss: memory (not measured — the view cache at 10⁶ emitters is ~24 MB, fine) and the tail frames (engine p99 at budget with 45 overflow frames in 30 s: the kick tail is real but deferred by design; if deferral fails at scale it re-becomes the constraint — the board already owns it).
