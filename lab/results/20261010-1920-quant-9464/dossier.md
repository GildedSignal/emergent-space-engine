# Dossier — the plane's capacity ladder (run 20261010-1920-quant-9464)

**Claim.** The shipped plane's population dial was capped at 400 members by a silent CLI clamp, and once lifted, the plane's 60 Hz capacity is ~10³ members — set by the readout (24× the engine's per-member cost) and by the frame's own extent rule, which hands members out above ~10³; neither the cache hierarchy nor parallelism gets a vote at any reachable size.

**Bottleneck.** Board item "Scale" / plane.md's unmeasured "events per second, bytes per body" at population. Expected gain: none built this run; this measures the ceiling the queued builds (`b-readout-cost`, `b-belt-wire`, `b-h-live`) move, and kills the idea that per-event compute (kicks, cache lines) is today's binding constraint at the plane's scale.

## Evidence

Machine: i7-4790 (Haswell) 4C/8T, 1 MiB L2/core, 8 MiB L3, load 2–5 (shared box), Linux. All timings are orders of magnitude for the M1; digests are exact. Instrument: the plane's own headless dashboard `xtask universe --bench`, unpaced (work per presented frame) unless said. Reproduce: `cargo build --release -p xtask`, apply this run's `work/patch.diff` (lifts `--members` clamp 400 → 2²¹), then `bash lab/results/20261010-1920-quant-9464/sweep2.sh` (every run ≤ 15 s). Raw JSONs beside this file; table in `table.md`.

1. **Instrument artifact (found, fixed, disclosed).** `xtask/src/universe/mod.rs:32` clamped `--members` to `clamp(4, 400)`: every rung above 400 silently measured the same 400-member universe (identical digests for members 512/1024/1536 in the first sweep, `n*.json`). All recorded plane numbers (p99 2.45–2.72 ms, H0210/H0211) are 400-member universes.
2. **The extent wall (the frame's size rule, |x| ≤ r_p/32 ≈ 148Q).** Leavers appear at 1024 members (4 handed out), 75 at 1300, 572 at 2048 (`bodies` column; predicted range 1024–1300 from the grid arithmetic 20Q + 5·√(n/2)·Q). The dense order caps at ~10³ members per frame at Home's low orbit; capacity grows as r_p² (a frame at the moon's distance would hold ~5×10⁶).
3. **Readout vs engine per member (rtf 1, in-frame, 48→1024).** Marginal cost ≈ 3.4 µs/member/frame, of which engine 0.14 µs (4%), gather 0.34 µs, JSON 2.3 µs, wire 0.64 µs — **the readout is ~24× the engine**. The binding stage at presentation is serialisation, then the gather.
4. **Past the wall the plane degrades fast (rtf 1).** A handed-out member becomes a catalog body the gather Newton-solves every frame: ~12 µs/body/frame (gather p50 0.58 → 7.33 ms from 400 → 2048). Frame CPU p50: 1.8 ms (400) → 4.0 (1024) → 5.6 (1300) → 14.0 (2048) → 48.5 (8192, no longer a 60 Hz universe) → 24 p99/161 max (32768, handout storm). Flight regime (rtf 100, as Julien flies): p50 7.2 (400) → 11.8 (1024) → 14.8 ms, p99 22.2 (2048) — **the p99 SLA (16.7 ms) breaks between 1300 and 2048 members**.
5. **Threads and caches do not vote.** threads 4 vs 1 (members 1300, rtf 100): digest identical ✓, engine p50 5.5 vs 5.4 ms, p99 worse (33.8 vs 19.7) — the chunked kick pass is not the binding work. In-frame hot state at the wall (1300 × ~64 B ≈ 83 KB) sits in L1/L2; the L2 cliff (~10⁴ members) is unreachable inside one frame.
6. **Paced frames cost 2.4× unpaced** (m1024: p50 9.4 vs 4.0 ms, same digest): sleeping 16.7 ms between frames empties caches — the player's cadence taxes the readout; unpaced ladders understate presentation cost.
7. **Determinism controls (all pass).** Repeat runs byte-identical digests; paced = unpaced; threads 1 = 4; members 400 run matches the pre-patch clamped digest. `bytes_per_body` = 1196 B counts catalog bodies only (the fleet is one entity; members uncounted — instrument limit).

**Predictions** (`predictions.md`, written before the decisive sweep): P3 extent wall 1024–1300 ✓; P2 readout binds at 10³–10⁴ ✓ at rtf 1 (json first), engine ties it at rtf 100 ✓; P5 rtf scaling ✓; P6 controls ✓; P1 cache cliff — untestable in-frame (the wall is 10³, the cliff 10⁴: the representation binds first) — held as untestable, not confirmed; P4 threads ≥2× engine gain ✗ (no gain; the pass is not the bottleneck). First sweep voided by the 400 clamp and disclosed.

**What would make it wrong.** The ladder runs one scene (two closing fleets at Home's low orbit) and one seed; a designed capacity scene (spread formations, higher orbits) moves the wall as r_p² but should not move the 24× readout/engine ratio. If the dashboard's `events_per_second` (61/s at rtf 1) undercounts battle events, the engine share at rtf 1 is even smaller than reported — the claim's direction survives. Co-tenant load adds ~10% run-to-run scatter (m1024 vs dup: 3.99/3.68 ms); all conclusions are ≥2× effects.

**Cost of adoption, fit.** Adoption is one line (the clamp) plus three ceiling numbers for queued builds: the readout must become O(visible emitters), not O(bodies), to carry a belt (b-readout-cost now has its target: 3.4 µs/member/frame caps one core near 5×10³ presented members at 60 Hz; 10⁹ bodies need culling plus aggregates, never per-body serialisation); belts are not one battle (b-belt-wire: the wall is ~10³ at low orbit, r_p² higher); the 2.4× paced tax says readout caching across frames is worth measuring on the M1. PRINCIPLES: "minimize spike compute and average compute usage" (12) — measured as p99/worst per rung, not means; "Compute only surprises" (3) — the gather re-derives every body every frame, the one place the plane still pays for what nobody asked to see; "a body resolves only the influences significant at its own scale" (33) — the extent rule is that principle working as designed, and it, not compute, chooses the representation at 10³.

**The lens (what it may have missed).** An HFT lens prices tails per frame but underweights exactness: the digest invariance across budget, pacing and threads is the plane's real asset, and no p99 number should buy a schedule that breaks it. Also, one scene, one seed, one shared box: behaviours over many seeds (20) were not climbed here, and wall-time hitches from the co-tenants were excluded by using CPU-time percentiles — a real player hitch that is a stall, not compute, would be invisible to this instrument.

**Grade (author's self-assessment): silver.** Real, reproduced, controls both sides, one instrument bug found and fixed with disclosure; not gold because nothing 10× was adopted — it hands `b-readout-cost` and `b-belt-wire` their ceiling numbers and the integrator a one-line clamp decision.
