Reproduced with interleaved A/B (5 alternating reps per tree): medians +10.2–14.5% on all five dense tracks, every dense rep positive; dilute tracks +5–10% and noisy; the engine's cost test passes, gain ≈0. **Why silver, not gold.** It moves no bottleneck 10×, deletes nothing, and overturns no premise — the author says so. And the held-out magnitude predictions, the actual scientific claims, all failed; lab precedent (1 October red-team) grades that silver at best. Goal fit is PRINCIPLES 16 and 13 — same function, same bits, cheaper arithmetic — but the gain concentrates on contact-dense CPU tracks, which the barbell (H0170's mode split) hands to Metal on the M1, while EventCore's own sparse domain gains a noisy 5–10% and the real consumer (the plane's cost test) measures ≈0.

**The experiment that would make it gold.** The profile's own map: a covering certificate that batches re-predictions — one sweep over a cell's pairs instead of per-body neighbour visits (the 43%) — or a calendar without sift-down on every later-set (14–16%). A structural ≥10× cut in visits per fired event (or deletion of the per-neighbour loop) at unchanged digests is gold-sized.

**What the author missed.** The prediction model was priced from i128 source, not from generated code — a one-minute objdump of main's predicate would have calibrated it; the lesson they wrote afterwards was knowable before. The adoption case was argued on dense tracks where the architecture routes to the GPU, not on the sparse prediction-dominated domain where EventCore actually lives and where the gain is weakest. The 43%/16% profile split is unverified here (gprofng exists on this machine; not run within budget) — the next run must re-derive it before building on it. The bottleneck citation is loose: HANDOFF's "Headed next 5" is encounters-at-N and populations at scale; contact-engine ns/event is a defensible reading, not the item.
