# 20261010-2314-library-e5a2 — the library lens on kick cadence

**Claim.** Julien's library says the tide-kick layer should pay on *crossing of a quantum*, not on a period: measured on a toy belt, a body whose compiled drift carries the secular model can defer essentially all tide evaluations (500+ orbits, 100% of mild-e far bodies) until the accumulated un-modelled residue crosses the element quantum — so `q-cadence` should be built as a per-body deferral certificate, not a swept fixed cadence.

**Bottleneck.** Board focus #1, "fewer kicks per orbit; kicks dominate the engine's cost"; the taste log already says the lever is cadence and `lab/queue.txt` holds `q-cadence` (120 min). Expected gain: the tide half of the kick layer deletes for most of a belt (order 10–100× fewer tide evaluations), conditional on one quantum choice.

## The five lessons (all quotes verbatim from his Readwise export, cached in `readwise-full.json` / `highlights.tsv`)

1. **Pay on crossing, not on period.** "Over a short time increment, one observes the variability of the portfolio, not the returns… one sees the variance, little else." (Taleb, *Fooled by Randomness*); "The more frequently you look at data, the more noise you are disproportionally likely to get." (Taleb, via Farnam Street's 20 Aphorisms). Mechanism: a near-zero-mean fluctuation sampled at its natural frequency costs per sample and yields nothing at the decision quantum. Change: `q-cadence`'s representation — per body, carry a bound on accumulated un-applied tide residue (amplitude from elements alone); evaluate the tide only when that bound crosses the element quantum. Cheapest test: the probe below (run).
2. **Delete, with add-back as the metric.** "Delete any part or process you can. You may have to add them back later. In fact, if you do not end up adding back at least 10% of them, then you didn't delete enough." (Isaacson, *Elon Musk*). Mechanism: irreversible-looking deletions are made reversible by measuring the add-back rate. Change: method.md's measured-dead candidates (`shipmem`, `encounters`, ~4,500 unused lines) get deleted now with add-backs logged; and the kick **window setup** (92% of per-kick evals, gold 20261006-0737) gets *deleted* (folded into the once-per-element-change certificate), not deferred — deferral was already killed. Cheapest test: cut the setup path; count re-added setups per 1,000 real encounters (distinctness-checked, per the 5 Oct artifact lesson).
3. **Bet the law is as good as it is.** "I've very often made mistakes in my physics by thinking the theory isn't as good as it really is, thinking that there are lots of complications that are going to spoil it." (Feynman, *Surely You're Joking*). Mechanism: preemptive correction machinery pays for complications that never spoil the certificate — the six "correctors/higher-order splits buy nothing" runs were exactly this mistake. Change: each queued build first falsifies "the simple law already passes": before `b-plunge-tubes`, check whether B13's four marginal step-7 failures sit in the oracle's domain (B12/B13/B17 oracles all had domain errors before). Cheapest test: rerun B13's 4 failures against a verified-domain oracle; if they pass, the tube build defers.
4. **The merge certificate is Mediocristan.** "When your sample is large, no single instance will significantly change the aggregate or the total." (Taleb, *The Black Swan*). Mechanism: an aggregate is safe exactly when no single member can move it by one quantum — a dimensionless max-to-total test, not a body count. Change: H_live (unmeasured, top risk) gets its instrument: per game-second, count distinctions whose *single* removal changes any gate-visible observable; that unmergeable fraction, not bytes×N, is the memory floor's measurement. Cheapest test: one Zipf-fleet scene, one game-second, the count above.
5. **Batch the readout at the consumer's quantum.** "Do not check e-mail constantly — 'batch' and check at set times only." (Tim Ferriss, *The Not-to-Do List*). Mechanism: coalescing per-event evaluation into per-window evaluation is free when the consumer's decision quantum is coarser than the event rate. Change: the queued readout build evaluates observables at frame quantum with per-observable dirty bits, never per event. Cheapest test: count, per frame, observables whose inputs moved less than one display quantum; if <5% recompute, the readout is free.

## Evidence (probe: lesson 1's cheapest test, run before any tuning past `predictions.md`)

Toy CR3BP (star 1, Jupiter 10⁻³ at 5.2 AU, 240 massless belt bodies a∈[2,3.6], half e₀=0.05 half e₀=0.2, planar, heliocentric with indirect term), leapfrog dt=P_p/300, 200 P_p ≈ 500 body orbits. Per body: orbit-averaged osculating a; model = 40-orbit moving average (stand-in for the compiled secular drift); residue r=ā−model; S=cumsum r. D(Q)=orbits until |S|>Q·a.

- Far-from-resonance, e₀=0.05: **D(10⁻⁴) = never crossed (censored, full horizon)** — 100% of these bodies can skip every tide evaluation.
- Far, e₀=0.2 (adversarial): D(10⁻⁴)=5 orbits, **D(10⁻³)=477** — a cliff in Q, not a gentle slope.
- All bodies pooled (far): 49% censored at Q=10⁻⁴, 89% at 10⁻³. Residue growth γ (windowed |S|): median −0.05, p90 +0.12 — bounded everywhere reached.
- **Prediction 2 failed:** bodies flagged by proximity to p:q resonances (|a−a_res|<0.04 AU, 25% of belt) show no pumping here (γ p90 = 0.12, D(10⁻⁴)=141): spatial proximity does not separate classes; the resonance that kills averaging (H0203, r4) must be identified by the compiled-frequency condition with libration, not a spatial flag. The deferral certificate still needs that dimensionless trigger; this toy never entered the librating regime.
- Deferral is a **cliff at Q ≈ residue amplitude** (per-orbit residue scatter ≈1.7×10⁻³·a): below it, per-orbit payment; above it, payment disappears. The element quantum, not any algorithm, decides the tide layer's cost.

Commands (each ≲ 30 s): `cd lab/results/20261010-2314-library-e5a2/work && python3 probe.py` (raw, linear-fit model — kept for the record) and `python3 probe2.py` (smoothing-window model; numbers above; both write `probe_out.json`). Floats explore; no canonical outcome claimed.

**Predictions:** see `predictions.md` (written before the probe). Held: 1 (bounded far residue, D≥10 at 10⁻⁴), 3 (e₀=0.2 bounded, D≥5 at 10⁻⁴), 4 (cliff in Q). Failed: 2 (near-resonance pumping absent in this toy — reported above, and it sharpens the trigger design rather than killing the rule).

**What would make it wrong.** The model stand-in (moving average) is not the engine's compiled secular drift; if the real generator's residual has correlated structure at tens of orbits, D collapses — re-measure with the actual compiled drift on belt 0. Near-resonance and high-e belts are unmeasured in the librating regime. Adoption cost: one per-body residue-amplitude bound (from elements, closed-form) plus the quantum choice; it composes with the existing re-anchor rule (law.md "Not adopted" — same crossing shape, already graded gold once).
