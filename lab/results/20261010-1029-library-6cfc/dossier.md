**Claim.** The board's top bottleneck (the slack skin, kicks' window setup) needs its neighbour set to be **phase-rank, not metric**: measured, a metric slack skin under co-orbital concentration saturates to N candidates per body (300–809 vs 2.5–16 in a belt) and 50–80× the rebuild work, while a k=4 phase-rank skin is immune (3 candidates per body everywhere, 14× *fewer* rebuilds than belt); and the tempting B12 instrument built from Taleb's maxim (judge by maxima, not averages) **loses, measured**, to the window-mean slope (AUC 0.920 vs 0.967 on rare-actuating leaks; both blind at T=100), so B12's redesign should use window-mean slope with windows sized so E[actuations] ≳ 10.

**Bottleneck.** Board focus #1 (fewer kicks per orbit; the 6 Oct gold named the slack skin 12.6× the lever) plus the board's top scaling risk (candidate work must track real encounters). Gain sought: keep the 12.6× under the adversarial workload instead of losing it to concentration; delete a wrong instrument before it is built.

- Formation kills the *metric* skin through candidate-set saturation (every body's window contains the whole packed fleet), the same disease the taleb-09b5 gold found in the window tier — it reappears one layer down. No slack size m escapes it (all m: 300–809 vs belt's 2.5–16).
- In a belt, once any skin exists (m ≥ 1.5) setup work is already ~1e-3 and pair-evals dominate; total cost *rises* with m — slack beyond ~1.5 spacings buys nothing at setup:eval cost ratios up to 11 (the 92%-setup regime). Slack amortization is real (setups fall 6.7× from m 1.5→8) but it is not where the remaining loss lives.
- Phase-rank (k=4, the gold's partner-rank-4 fact) is strictly better under concentration and equal in belts. The skin's *shape*, not its *size*, is the decision.

B12 probe (bounded AR(0.98) with finite-variance heavy-tailed noise vs same + 1%-actuating jumps; 200 seeds; threshold-free AUC): leak at T=1000 — max-slope AUC 0.920, mean-slope 0.967, RMS 0.960; at T=100 all ≈ 0.50 (one jump in 100 steps is invisible to every statistic tried). Always-on leaks separate at T=1000 for all statistics (0.99+). The v1 probe used student-t df=2 noise (infinite variance): its running max swamped the leak; reported as a probe-design flaw, v2 fixed it.

## The five lessons (practitioners with skin in the game; quotes verbatim from the Readwise export, `readwise.json`)

1. **Skin shape over skin size** — *"So I got a great reputation for doing integrals, only because my box of tools was different from everybody else's, and they had tried all their tools on it before giving the problem to me."* (Feynman, "Surely You're Joking"). Mechanism: tuning m is effort inside the metric toolbox; the phase-rank set is a different tool. Change: `b-encounter-skin` builds the neighbour set as k nearest in phase wherever the concentration number fires; add the formation case to its gate (today the gate sweeps ε on belts only). Test: this probe's formation rows (metric fails, phase holds).
2. **Slack is an option whose value scales with surprise** — *"Redundancy is ambiguous because it seems like a waste if nothing unusual happens. Except that something unusual happens—usually."* (Taleb, Antifragile). Mechanism: slack pays where neighbour flux is high; concentration is the usual unusual. Change: size slack per regime from measured flux, never one global m. Test: probe 1, belt vs formation columns.
3. **Local efficiency lies** — *"When people are used to evaluating their productivity locally, they feel that a good day is one in which they did their job well all day."* (Ries, The Lean Startup). Mechanism: the 92% setup cost was invisible while per-kick work looked lean; the skin's gate must count setups **and** pair-evals end-to-end. Change: `b-encounter-skin`'s gate adds pair-evals/kick/body next to setups/kick. Test: probe 1's third column.
4. **Delete with an expected add-back; automate last** — *"Delete any part or process you can. You may have to add them back later. In fact, if you do not end up adding back at least 10% of them, then you didn't delete enough."* and *"Always wait until the end of designing a process—after you have questioned all the requirements and deleted unnecessary parts—before you introduce automation."* (Musk, in Isaacson, Elon Musk). Mechanism: deletion is an experiment with a resurrector, and automation freezes waste. Change: execute the 7 Oct deletion audit (shipmem, encounters) with a tag to resurrect, and hold the allocator build until the skin and deletion land, then re-measure. Test: gates green after deletion; per-kick cost re-measured before allocator code exists.
5. **When a maxim and a measurement disagree, the measurement wins** — *"By a law of probability called distribution of the maximum of random variables, the maximum of an average is necessarily less volatile than the average maximum."* (Taleb, Fooled by Randomness). Mechanism: the maxim says judge by maxima; on rare-actuating leaks the window-mean slope separates better (0.967 vs 0.920) and *nothing* works at T=100. Change for `b12-v2`/`q-b12-design`: statistic = slope of window-mean drift across ≥4 sub-windows, window sized so E[actuations] ≳ 10; report separation (AUC-style) across seeds, never a single-seed threshold. Test: probe 2 v2.

**What would make this wrong.** The skin probe is 1-D phase ring, one rate spread, jitter 100× below slack; real fleets have eccentricity/inclination dispersion and station-keeping burns, which raise phase-skin rebuild rates (bounded by k, so it degrades to belt levels, not worse — but unmeasured here). The B12 probe is synthetic: the real leak's actuation rate and jump shape are unknown; if actuations are frequent but tiny the ranking could flip. Cloud numbers are Linux/x86, orders of magnitude only.

**Cost of adoption.** Small: the phase-rank set reuses the taleb-09b5 certificate (rank ≤ 4) inside `crates/encounters`; the B12 change is a statistic choice in the gate redesign already in flight (`q-b12-design`, `b12-v2`). No crate or gate is edited here.

**Fit with PRINCIPLES.** "Compute only surprises… Nothing is evolved that can be derived when looked at" (P3): the formation's candidates are exactly the non-surprises a metric skin re-derives every kick. "A fix signals the wrong representation" (P19): enlarging m is the bandaid; the phase-rank set is the boat. "Measure before claiming" (P10) and the test-ladder: both falsified predictions are kept, not buried.
