# Five lessons for the cadence bottleneck (q-cadence), from Julien's library

Bottleneck: board #1 — kicks dominate the engine's cost; the lever is cadence.
All quotes verbatim, retrieved from the Readwise export (cached: `rw-page-*.json`).

1. **Delete before you automate.** "Always wait until the end of designing a
   process—after you have questioned all requirements and deleted unnecessary
   parts—before you introduce automation." — Walter Isaacson, *Elon Musk*.
   Mechanism: automation multiplies whatever remains, waste included; the cheapest
   kick is the one never delivered. Change: q-cadence's rule must first route
   predictable bodies to the zero-kick averaged layer (bounded by the resonance ban
   and the unpredictability wall), and only then pick finite rates — the raw layer
   is at its cliff (quant-5022). Test (ran): per-body tuning alone caps at 1.92×
   even-weighted on quiet hierarchical seeds, so the 10× must come from deletion.

2. **The sensor, not the actuator, is the hard part.** "A Common homeostatic
   behavior in humans is temperature regulation. If the temperature rises above the
   98.6 F optimum..., sensors on the skin detect it and signal the brain that a rise
   has occurred." — Farnam Street, "Mental Model: Feedback Loops".
   Mechanism: a thermostat is a local sensor plus a thresholded actuator; when the
   sensor saturates, switching harder buys nothing. Change: cadence is a ×2 ladder
   switched at a body's own events by a *local unpredictability* sensor; inside a
   resonance band the actuator is wrong — change law layer, not rate. Test (ran):
   on quant-5022's per-seed data, no recorded number (e0 ρ=+0.36, a0 +0.67, μ +0.27)
   orders the needed rate; the missing sensor inputs are τ and resonance distance.

3. **Binary rules are stabler.** "Outcomes are paradoxically more stable under the
   minority rule—the variance of the results is lower... What emerges from the
   minority rule is more likely to be black-and-white, binary rules." — Nassim
   Taleb, *Skin in the Game*. Mechanism: thresholds do not interpolate, so input
   jitter cannot drift the output. Change: the cadence rule emits only power-of-two
   steps with hysteresis (the tie-rule precedent), decided exactly at events — the
   law's schedule-independence constraint. Test: every seed's minimum sits on the ×2
   grid; a graded rate adds no pass on this data (probe P4).

4. **Judge the maximum, not the average.** "By a law of probability called
   distribution of the maximum of random variables, the maximum of an average is
   necessarily less volatile than the average maximum." — Nassim Taleb, *Fooled by
   Randomness*. Mechanism: aggregating before the extremum hides the tail the player
   feels (Julien: "i care about the p95/99"). Change: q-cadence's gold must pair
   total kicks with worst-frame kick maxima over the median seed; a rule that halves
   totals but doubles the worst frame regresses. Test (ran): one seed (H1, a 0.62)
   pins the uniform rate at 64 while the mean seed accepts ~19 — the "uniform cliff"
   is the max at work; gains must be reported max-aware.

5. **Surprise is the barometer — for compute and memory alike.** "Capture Criteria
   #4: Is It Surprising? Surprise is an excellent barometer for information that
   doesn't fit neatly within our existing understanding, which means it has the
   potential to change how we think." — Tiago Forte, *Building a Second Brain*.
   Mechanism: one criterion — misfit with the current model — decides both what to
   store and what to compute. Change: cadence's setpoint is denominated in
   distinctions, not error: a kick is due when generator-truth divergence reaches
   one observable quantum; the same counter is H_live's budget (b-h-live). Test:
   the 5 October memo method with distinct-kick accounting, applied per body.
