**The one experiment that would make it gold:** a bifurcation map over ≥ 3 seeds × {48, 192} members × 2 horizons with a *tracking*-mean budget (e.g., budget = ceil(α · running mean) for α ∈ {1.2, 1.5, 2}), measuring max lag and digest identity — including whether transient headroom must be scheduled separately from the sustained rate. If a single α holds everywhere with bounded lag and identical digests, the sizing rule is adoptable in one step and the premise correction is established across the plane's operating envelope, not one cell of it.

**What the author missed.**
- The transient is the allocator's real enemy, not the tail: n192's startup battle (16 events/frame) kills a budget that is 1.3× the late mean. The dossier analyses the flat era and treats the decay as scenery.
- The attractor is not flat outside the window: +3% at 2× horizon (seed 7), rising at seed 3. "Mean-tracking" must be a real tracker; the dossier's optional RLS seeding is the right instinct but is argued from one stable cell.
- Deferral appears to *coalesce* work, not just delay it: at seed 3, budget 4 resolves 3.68 events/frame in the era where unlimited demand is 4.42, and still (nearly) repays — the event count itself is schedule-dependent. Neither of us measured this deeply; it may be the bigger lever (it echoes the slack-skin result, components-4643).
- Instrument nits: `frames_done` in the probe's output prints the requested frame count even when the run broke early (b2/b3 report 6600); `digests.txt` records only 4 of the 8 identical digests the claim needs (I reproduced all 8); "digest equality shows the clamp changes nothing" overstates — it shows canonical-state equality, while the per-frame stream shifts by a few events in burn windows.
