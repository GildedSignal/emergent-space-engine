The five lessons (each: verbatim quote → mechanism → change → cheapest test):

1. **Inversion** — Munger, via Farnam Street ("The Munger Operating System"):
   *"problems frequently get easier and I would even say usually \*are\* easier
   to solve if you turn around in reverse. … you think 'what's doing the worst
   damage in India? What would automatically do the worst damage and how do I
   avoid it?' You'd think they are logically the same thing, but they're not."*
   Mechanism: proving pairs can never meet is a birth constant (fixed relative
   phase ⇒ fixed min-distance); finding pairs that do is quadratic and
   recurring. Change: charge fleet contact capability at birth and train pairs
   on `dioph.rs::first_hit` (exists, unwired); the standing rebuild dies.
   Test: on the crate's coherent verify fleets, birth-constant min-distances
   must explain all-but-answer pairs of the paid set (2373: 54 of every 55).
2. **Entrants, not survivors** — Taleb, *Fooled by Randomness*: *"For one
   cannot consider a profession without taking into account the average of the
   people who enter it, not the sample of those who have succeeded in it."*
   Mechanism: composition survivorship — a ledger of measured rows omits the
   unmeasured population. Change: the 10⁹ CPU ledger becomes a population
   table (belt / dolls / fleets), headline = the sum at the worst mix.
   Test: `compose_ledger.py` (one command).
3. **Information manufactures hypotheses** — Taleb, *The Black Swan*: *"The
   more information you give someone, the more hypotheses they will formulate
   along the way, and the worse off they will be. They see more random noise
   and mistake it for information."* Mechanism: the standing horizon H grows
   paid pairs linearly (k̄ ∝ H·da) while the answer is rank-0 invariant —
   7.4×10⁶ paid pairs per contact is noise mistaken for information. Change:
   delete the standing horizon; event-scheduled Diophantine due dates (f_r =
   0.017-0.025 vs 5-7 per-refresh, 277d). Test: 2373's H-arm extended —
   pre-registered expectation: answer rank-0 at every H (refusal H* caps H
   anyway).
4. **Wisdom, not ignorance** — Asimov, *The Naked Sun*: *"I could not bring
   myself to believe that if knowledge presented danger, the solution was
   ignorance. … You did not refuse to look at danger, rather you learned how
   to handle it safely."* Mechanism: the dial's coarse end deletes the fleet
   row (4,500 → 4×10⁻⁵ cores) by refusing same-size conjunctions (49 m ≪ D*
   1.6 km) — but P3 shows it also blinds the only fleet finder. Change: set ev
   from observables (what the player differentiates; PRINCIPLES #30), delete
   the fleet row by representation (lesson 1), and treat the dial as the
   biggest single encounter decision (4.5×10⁵×), not as tuning. Test: the
   contact-delivery probe at ev = −45 — does any shipped path find a 49-m
   fleet hit without the standing certificate? (None today.)
5. **Rate-determining step** — Young, *Ultralearning*: *"The rate-determining
   step is the slowest part of this chain of reactions, forming a bottleneck
   that ultimately defines the amount of time needed for the entire reaction to
   occur."* Mechanism: at the fine end the fleet row is 99.77% of composed
   CPU; skin (12.6×) and gate (24×) move ≤ 2.3% of the total. Change: the
   encounters queue's next rung is the fleet row (representation or dial),
   nothing else. Test: the lever table in `compose_ledger.py`.
