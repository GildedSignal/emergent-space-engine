## What would make it wrong

the held-answer store is already on a deletion list (0e37's two-word completion deletes the view's ramp reads, not the
scan's held answers), or if the retaining site turns out to be prof-only (the stage timers wrap `capture` — but the
kill reproduces without prof in the batch run? **not checked: both killed runs had prof on**; the checker should rerun
one kill arm with prof off), the ruin shrinks to the 615 ms/frame cost term. The coast monster (2,008 ms) and the
214-1,087× scan costs stand regardless. If `plane_extra_band`'s `None` arm is not bit-for-bit `plane_extra`, the
controls are contaminated — check: `plane_with_extra` delegates to `plane_built(.., None)` with the original player
spec in the `None` arm (scenario.rs, this run's patch).

## Cost of adopting, and fit with PRINCIPLES

Adoption is nothing yet: this is a measurement, not a fix. The fix direction the numbers point to is the engine's own
idiom already named: the scan's keep set must carry its own scale (7f99's "dyadic widening level per interval class")
and the held answers must not retain per re-plan; bf21's pull cap does not touch this (the coast monster is the pull,
the ruin is the scan). PRINCIPLES #12 ("the allocator… the most important one") and #25 ("not making mistakes matters
more than doing things efficiently, don't multiply by zero"): a player who flies into the belt today multiplies by
zero. #2 (behaviours matter): the game's promise is a flyable belt; the seam sits exactly on it.

## The lens

Taleb asks what breaks: this found the concavity (scan cost per call ∝ belt population at fixed density → superlinear
in N) and the ruin state (memory death, not latency). What it may have missed: the exact retaining site (via negativa
wants a deletion, not a diagnosis), and whether a cheaper scene (player crossing the band on a transfer, not parked in
it) hits the same wall — the parked player is the worst case, which is the lens's habit, but adoption needs the
transit case too.
