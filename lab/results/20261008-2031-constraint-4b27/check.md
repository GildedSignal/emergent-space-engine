**Why silver, not gold.**
1. **Held-out prediction 3 did not hold**, on two of three components: direction 1.126e-4 against a 1e-4 bound (13% over), light-seconds 1.09e-4 against 1e-5 (one order). The author's own pre-registered kill criterion — "any sampled display error beyond the bounds in 3 … kills the mechanism as built" — was therefore triggered but not invoked; the ls re-derivation (position error enters ls at full relative size, not the distance ratio) is plausible but post-hoc. Honest, and not gold.
2. **My adversarial cases.** (a) 5400 frames, rtf 100: max_dir 1.675e-4, max_rel_ls 1.641e-4 — past the claimed ~1e-4 tolerance; worst offender is the *planet* Home at frame 4900, i.e. a long arc between chain events, exactly the case the one-probe constant-curvature cap underestimates. The dossier's control only bounds 1800 frames. (b) 240 members: error stays ≤ ~1e-4, readouts 24 at 169 seen — scales. (c) rtf 400: the cap binds (over_cap 2, readouts 7), error ~1e-4 — the span mechanism works. So the gate is sound; its bound is not yet a bound.
3. **The 7× headline mixes machine states.** The stored baseline bench is uniformly ~4.7× slower than my identical rerun (engine p99 5.25 vs 1.36 ms; overflow 46 vs 3 frames) — a whole-run slowdown of that leg, not a gather property. Matched-state today: frame p50 0.94→0.30 ms (3.1×), gather p50 0.73→0.11 ms (6.9×). The dossier flags machine-state dependence and wisely leans on shares and readouts — but the claim line, dossier evidence and author-final all lead with 7×.
4. **Adoption is not one step**: it requires the integrator to relax the frozen 20261003 cache-purity contract (bit-equality → display tolerance), i.e. a decision on a frozen gate, plus the cap fix.
