# Dossier — 20261011-0736-components-825f (components lens: the frame allocator)

**Claim.** The plane's frame allocator is already at its floor — the idiot index is 1.01–1.03 (engine p99 spend = p99 mandatory demand) because **99.6% of the engine's work has zero light-cone slack** (the whole event stream is the battle and the player, both at the player; ships on plain orbits resolve no items at all), so there is nothing to defer and nothing to build in the scheduler: at 428 bodies the mandatory demand itself is over the frame's engine budget (p99 6.0 ms steady-state, 45 ms in the battle spinup, against 5 ms), which stalls the presented time (159/1800 overflow frames, real-time factor lowest-1% = 0.0) while frame times never hitch — and the demand tail is set by item costs the queue cannot see, led by the player's own motion-law step, measured **O(members of its doll)** (337 µs at 48 members → 1,560 µs at 400, the per-source loop `crates/universe/src/ext.rs:149` fed by `universe.rs:427`).

**Bottleneck.** Board #4 (smoothness and the allocator; Julien: "the algorithm that allocates the work to prevent those heavy tails, it's the most important one", PRINCIPLES 12) and HANDOFF's risk list: constraint-986f asserted "the engine's real cost is a p99 burst problem the allocator already owns" — this run measures it and finds the opposite: the allocator owns nothing because it has no freedom. Expected gain: deletes the "better scheduler" branch (no build), and names the two demand-side levers with line numbers — the O(sources) kick loop (the board's top scaling risk, O(N²), alive in the plane's own kick layer) and the fleet battle slice as one item of up to 2.8 ms.

**Evidence.** All from `lab/results/20261011-0736-components-825f/work/` (GitHub Actions Linux, 8 vCPU, load 1.0–1.4, quiet; Linux/x86 timings are orders of magnitude only). The trace (`ES_ALLOC_TRACE=…` on `xtask universe --bench`, patches in the run's `work/patch.diff`) logs every resolved item's tick, deadline and cost; **mandatory** = deadline ≤ the frame's target (its light already reaches the player). Digest at the canonical cut is identical traced vs untraced vs `--engine-ms 10` (`3db02d3d…`): instrumentation and budget move *when* work happens, never *what* (foundation 7's contract, verified on this scene).

- **Idiot index 1.01** (m48 scene: 1.03). Deferrable share of engine time: **0.4%** (m48: 1.0%); deferrable slack: median 0.0 frames, max 0.1 (≈50,000 km of light travel; one frame advances 338,376 ticks). The light cone partitions nothing here.
- **Who has events:** player (entity 27; 478 ms, 23% of engine time), the fleet battle (entity 428; 210 ms, worst item 2.8 ms), and handed-out members (p50 46 µs). The 20 shipped ships on orbits (haulers, orbiters, skimmers) resolve **zero** items — pure compiled drift, the law working as designed. So all events are player-local by construction of the scene.
- **Demand by tick** (the floor any scheduler faces): steady-state (frames > 300) p50 0.04 / p99 6.03 / max 16.6 ms; the spinup (frames 12–21, the battle's first contact epochs) demands 20–45 ms per frame.
- **The stall, not the hitch:** 159/1800 frames overflow (mandatory work left undone), presented-time lowest-1% = **0.0** — the game freezes instants while frames stay ≤ 10.5 ms (zero hitches above 16.7 ms). The dashboard already carries this metric (`rtf.lowest_1pct`, `overflow_frames`) and it fails at 428 bodies.
- **The budget knob cannot reach smoothness:** at `--engine-ms 10` the overflows drop to 30 but rtf lowest-1% is still 19.8 and the engine's max reaches 10.9 ms (frame ≈ 14.4 of 16.7 with the view's ~3.5 ms) — the demand p99 (6–11 ms) exceeds both budgets; the knob trades stalls for hitch risk.
- **The player's step scales with the battle:** 337 µs/item at 48 members, 1,560 µs at 400 (slope 3.5 µs/member): the motion-law v2 kick loops over the doll's `sources` (`ext.rs:149`), and every member handed into the doll is pushed there (`universe.rs:427`). At 10⁶ members in one doll one player step is hours — the O(N²) candidate-pairs risk in its simplest form, before encounters even run.

**Predictions.** `predictions.md` was written before the traced runs. Held: P4 (rekey 24 µs/frame), P5 (the overshoot above budget is one item's granularity, player-shaped), P6 (digests identical, all three). Failed, and reported as failed: **P2** — deferrable share is 0.4%, not ≥ 70% (the compute-ahead reservoir is empty, not full); **P3** — slack is 0 frames, not ≥ 10 (there is no far work to defer: the orbiting ships generate no events at all, a possibility I had not listed); **P1 half-wrong** — the player is 20% of overflow-frames' mandatory work (1 item per frame, not several during burns); the dominant mechanism is the clustering of many cheap member items with the player's step and an occasional fat fleet slice.

**What would make it wrong.** If real play keeps the player away from the action: at 1 AU a body's deadline is ~198 frames out at rtf 100, and the light-cone scheduler gets real fuel — this scene (the benchmark's own scripted player, and the one Julien flies) pins the player inside the battle, the worst case, chosen deliberately. If the in-flight motion-law builds (b-energy-register, b-handoff-surface, the single-surface handoff) collapse item costs, the floor drops under the budget and the allocator holds — re-measure after they land. If the spinup were the whole tail (it is not: steady p99 6.0 ms > 5 ms budget). Adoption cost: none — no code to adopt; the finding redirects effort to the kick layer's doll-aggregate moment (one multipole source per doll instead of a per-body source list: principle 32, the symmetry is the doll) and to budget-sized battle slices. Fit with PRINCIPLES: 12 (the allocator is "the most important one" — measured at its floor, so the tails live in the items it is fed); 5 ("light cones partition compute" — measured: they partition nothing when the events are at the player); 1 (the cheapest compute is compute not done — the scheduler already does none); 3 (the cost follows the interactions clustering in time — chaos as "necessary interactions happening within a short time" is exactly the 45 ms spinup demand).

**The lens.** One component measured in isolation on one scene, one seed, one scripted player, threads = 1, unpaced, on a shared x86 box (orders of magnitude only; the integrator re-measures on the M1). The trace's own cost is two `Instant::now` per item (~20 ns, noise against a 46 µs median item). Not measured here: the view's tail (constraint-986f's numbers stand — the frame stays view-bound at the median, and my demand numbers are the engine's share only); the demand process at true scale (the O(sources) slope is two points, 48 and 400); whether a doll-aggregate moment source changes kick *outcomes* (it must not, but that is the next run's falsifier, not this one's). A local optimum can also hide behind the horizon rule: presented time = min deadline − 1 means a deferral policy would stall differently than the eager one — but with 0.4% deferrable work, no policy has room to differ, which is the finding.
