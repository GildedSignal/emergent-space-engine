# Dossier — 20261006-1354-constraint-df79 (lens: the constraint)

**Claim.** On the plane as it stands (single thread, rtf 100, the player's setting), the felt constraint is game-time fidelity, not the frame: at the 400-member clamp 21% of frames overflow the engine's 5 ms wall budget while the mean engine load is only a third of it — the constraint is spike-shaped, its largest single spike is the player's O(doll-population) encounter scan (3.4 ms per item at m400: 27% of engine time from 1.5% of items, one item = 68% of a frame's whole engine budget), and 4 threads make the overflow worse (389→505), not better.

**Bottleneck.** The board's focus line 1 ("fewer kicks per orbit … kicks dominate the engine's cost") and the handoff's four measured plane bottlenecks. This run does not add a fifth: it **orders** them by measurement and attaches numbers to the queued fixes. Expected gains: deleting the player scan (the queued Binet cone) removes 27% of engine time at m400 and the largest single item, and is the difference between the plane working at fleet scale and not (the scan is O(N): a 10⁶-member doll would ask seconds per player item). The slack skin attacks the ship-mean (the 283 µs item vs the 35 µs resolver measured here implies ~250 µs of window setup per item, reproducing components-4643's 92%); it does not touch the player spike. Neither is the allocator: 66% of the engine budget sits idle on average while the tail breaks — the spike must be pre-spent in idle time, not made cheaper alone.

1. **Smoothness holds; game time breaks.** One frame above 16.7 ms per 30 s run at m200/m400, zero above 33 ms (`hitches_per_gameplay_hour`), p99.9 ≤ 15.7 ms. But the worst 1% of frames present ~zero game time (`rtf.lowest_1pct` → 0.03 at m200, 0.0 at m400): a visible stutter of the clock, never a lie (the horizon clamps below the earliest unresolved deadline, universe.rs:819–830). The transition sits between 76 and 221 bodies on this box.
2. **The mean is innocent.** Engine mean load: 0.62 ms/frame at m48, 1.67 at m200, 1.70 at m400 — 34% of the 5 ms budget at the clamp, yet 21% of frames overflow. The p99/mean spike ratio ≈ 3.6 at m400 and ≈ 10 at m48. Unpaced (full duty cycle) the freeze tail persists (worst 1% rtf 0.004): the spike is intrinsic to work arrival, not an artifact of the paced 5 ms cap.
3. **The player item is the spike's king.** Its cost grows with the doll's population while every other item's falls: 1299 → 3040 → 3405 µs for members 48 → 200 → 400 (ship: 283 → 104). Code: `PlayerNeighbourhood::find` scans every body of the doll with a tube evaluation each (crates/universe/src/encounter.rs:53), and the player's item forces the fleets to its tick first (universe.rs:564–586). Counter-case: at m48/rtf 1000 the player item costs 1135 µs against 6.5× the ship traffic — it tracks doll population, not traffic.
4. **Parallelism is not the lever.** `--threads 4` at m400: overflow 389 → 505, ship µs/item 104 → 156 (overhead), player item still serial (2716 µs). The wave path parallelises ships only; the player gates the horizon alone.
5. **The forever-stepped battle is most of the kick work.** Kick bench (m48, rtf 100): resolver-only median 35.3 µs per distinct made-body kick (p99 64) — the M1's 29 µs holds in order — but leaver kicks are 68% of samples (793/1158) and costlier (median 92.1 µs). The full item costs ~283 µs (ship, m48): ~250 µs of window setup around a 35 µs resolver, independently reproducing components-4643's 92%. **Exploit / subordinate / elevate** (the lens's steps): *Exploit* the constraint as it is — the budget's idle 66% is the resource; spend it ahead of the spikes (leaver first plans, encounter windows — "use idle time to compute ahead", principle 17; the allocator Julien calls "the most important one"). *Subordinate* — already right: the view never starves the engine (different currency), deferral never moves a decision, the digest stays canonical. *Elevate* — not by threads (measured worse); delete the O(N) player scan (the queued Binet cone) and cut the ship window setup (the queued slack skin). **Where the constraint moves next:** cone + skin land → engine mean ~10× down → the binding constraint becomes the view gather (62–63% of frame time, unbudgeted, per-body ~14 µs at m400) against the 16.7 ms line as bodies pass ~10³, then the encounter kick clusters; at the billion-body goal the plane's slice constraint is not the constraint at all — `shipmem` and the encounters certificates own that, and this lens says nothing about them.

**What would make it wrong.** (a) The worst-1% rtf could include catch-up frames >100 in its denominator; the *stutter* claim stands (a frame presenting zero game time is felt), the "cannot keep up" reading would soften. (b) One seed (1), 30 s runs, one shared box at load ≈ 1: orders of magnitude only; the 200-member transition band will sit elsewhere on the M1. (c) The player's 3.4 ms is the whole item (scan + forced fleet pull + own kick), not a profiler trace of `find()` alone; the population-tracking counter-case bounds it but a `perf` trace would pin the split. (d) `threads 4` worsening could be this box's topology (shared L2, hyperthreads) — the M1's 4P cores may differ. (e) If the checker finds the overflow frames coincide with `prune()` (every 64th frame) rather than arrival clusters, the spike story needs re-deriving.
