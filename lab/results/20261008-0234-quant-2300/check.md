**The one experiment that would make it gold.** A throwaway bench variant that actually defers the refit: accumulate Δv exactly across k kicks in the integer state and refit the tube once per k (at a boundary or certificate break), then measure µs/kick against the behaviour gates (B13 lost-flyby rate, B17 resonance). A ≥5–10× per-kick cut with behaviour unchanged is the 10× on focus #1 and becomes the design; a behaviour break shows which certificate the deferral needs. Either result is gold-shaped.

**What the author missed.**
- The bench saturates: at 6 s / RTF 100, `--members 1600` silently replays the m400 kick set (identical digest) — the capacity curve's 400 endpoint is a bench artifact, not a chosen extreme. The claim survives (my 20 s run extends it), but the author did not notice the ceiling.
- Loose claim: "kick cadence and force cost — both are <2% of the bill." Cadence is a multiplier on the *number* of kicks, not a share of one; only the cheaper-force lever is killed by 1–2%. `q-cadence` remains a live lever on total work, and the dossier's own note (`q-secular-v2`: cadence has a floor) is the correct way to bound it.
- The v2 clock path — the law everything converges on — has 12 samples per run and one merged stage (quantize+rebind+project); its "60%" cannot be split, and the author's own last paragraph concedes the fleet-of-clock-ships measurement is missing. A measurement run aimed at the next design should have covered it.
- The p99 ≈ 4× median tail is measured but unexplained (passage fits vs deep rebind levels vs coarser words); the allocator work (§12) will need that split.
- Prior art uncited: `1932f6c`'s 8% fit-arithmetic gain both anticipates the finding (fit = cost center) and bounds the micro-optimization path; it belongs in the dossier's "what would make it wrong".
