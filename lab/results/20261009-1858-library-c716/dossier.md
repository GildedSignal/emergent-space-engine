## The five lessons (each: verbatim highlight → mechanism → change → cheapest test)

1. **Amortize by validity lifetime, not cadence.** *"Amortization is the process of spreading the cost of a resource investment over the estimated useful life of that investment."* — Josh Kaufman, The Personal MBA. Verlet skins and LSM compaction price a rebuild over its *useful life*; a tier's life ends at the kick/event that invalidates it. Change: the candidate tier pays once per element change; rebuild trigger = accrued change > skin. Test: count rebuilds vs real encounters on the belt scene (must be constant ratio).
2. **Cap work-in-progress; pull, don't push.** *"Limited work in progress improves efficiency by preventing overwhelming overhead."* — Cal Newport (podcast notes). Kanban/matching engines never compare all orders: the book holds k slots and pulls the next event from the top. Change: k slots per body in τ-order; evaluate only on pull. Test: `probe_timekey.py` (median rank 2–4; static keys: 99% miss).
3. **Size the window by the tail, not the mean.** *"By a law of probability called distribution of the maximum of random variables, the maximum of an average is necessarily less volatile than the average maximum."* — Nassim Taleb, Fooled by Randomness. Mean-sized windows overflow for 47% of bodies; p99-sized for 1%. Change: k from the per-scene load's p99; misses shrink as N grows (self-averaging), so the gate is the tail at the *worst* scene, not the average N. Test: overflow table in `probe-out.txt`.
4. **Delete the enumeration; add back the 10%.** *"Delete any part or process you can. You may have to add them back later. In fact, if you do not end up adding back at least 10% of them, then you didn't delete enough."* — Walter Isaacson, Elon Musk. Cut to rank-k (O(N·k) memory, no pair storage), then buy back the overflow with a targeted second tier where misses concentrate (bursts; likely the high-e tail — untested). Discovery is the multiplicative part (*"understanding when you're in an additive system versus a multiplicative system"* — Farnam Street): a miss multiplies by zero, so the add-back must be certified zero-miss, while dynamics stay rough. Test: frac≤8 = 0.86–0.97 measured; add-back cost ≈ N × few.
5. **The bottleneck moves; re-measure or certify the past.** *"You would think that 3D printers would have solved the scarcity problem. In fact, the technology had just moved the bottleneck."* — Dennis E. Taylor, We Are Legion (cf. "a system will never grow beyond its biggest constraint"). The 6 Oct gold already found window setup at 92% after an adoption. Change: every rung report emits a before/after top-3 cost-center delta, or it grades no higher than silver. Test: re-run one adopted rung with the delta readout.

**The lens may have made me miss:** analogies are cheap; the toy has no selectivity, no eccentric/resonant truth, and no maintenance-cost measurement; the library is thin on practitioners who actually built matching engines, so mechanisms 1–2 lean on domain knowledge beyond the quotes.
