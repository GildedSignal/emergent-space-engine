# Dossier — 20261006-0916-library-80a8 (library lens)

**Claim.** Five practitioner lessons from Julien's own Readwise library change live engine decisions (kick setup removal, re-ranking after every fix, strain-triggered cadence, displacement-sized slack, small-batch allocation), and the probe on the load-bearing one shows that at belt density, phase-neighbour certificates cannot be maintained by element-change or quantum-slack payments at all: with **zero kicks** they still miss 100 % of close conjunctions — the churn is shear (displacement), so the skin must live in relative phase, not in element quanta.

**Bottleneck.** The board's #1 live question (kicks dominate engine cost) and the top scaling risk (certificate/candidate work must track real encounters). The plane's measured costs: kick 29 µs of which 15 µs is rebind/compile; view gather 83 % of a light frame; encounter check 190 µs/item. Gains aimed at: setup deletion on the kick (≈2×), certificate maintenance that tracks encounters (≥10× at zero misses), p99 frames. What it deletes: the rebind on the hot path; "each pair paid once per element change" as the maintenance law in shear-dominated regions.

## The five lessons (each: verbatim highlight → mechanism → change → cheapest test)

1. **Delete past comfort; expect to add back 10 %.** — *"Delete any part or process you can. You may have to add them back later. In fact, if you do not end up adding back at least 10 % of them, then you didn't delete enough."* (Elon Musk, Walter Isaacson.) Mechanism: deletion has a calibration dial — too-safe deletion shows up as add-backs; if nothing comes back, the pass was timid. Change: the kick's 15 µs rebind/compile is a part to **delete** (memoize the compiled fit per quantized fit-key; a hit is exact, integer), not to speed up; and the view gather (83 % of a light frame) should first **cull emitters below the player's perceptual floor**, then cache only what survives. Test: kick bench, second call on an unchanged fit — its cost is the rebind-free floor (29 µs → expect ≈14 µs); light-frame cost with floor-cull only, then cull+cache.
2. **Removing a constraint moves it; don't calculate the optimum, measure.** — *"In fact, the technology had just moved the bottleneck. … The calculations to determine the optimum path were finicky and had large error bars."* (We Are Legion, Dennis Taylor.) Mechanism: deconstraining one stage exposes the next, and optimum-mix calculations carry large error bars (Julien: "the simulation tells us what's next not us"). Change: re-rank the plane's four bottlenecks by a 60-s `--bench` after **every** merge (the 5 Oct ranking is already stale: fixing `step_for` alone takes the exponent 0.795 → 0.245); never start the next cost job from the old list. Test: `cargo run --release -p xtask -- universe --bench` before/after each merge, ranking updated from output.
3. **Two systems: strain triggers the expensive one, and it keeps the last word.** — *"…System 2 takes over when things get difficult, and it normally has the last word."* (Thinking, Fast and Slow, Daniel Kahneman.) Mechanism: cognitive economy — a cheap parallel automatic layer carries routine, an effortful serial layer is invoked by measured strain. Change (q-cadence): the tube/generator runs free between events (System 1); the **only** kick trigger is an integer strain test (the multipole residual's divergence crossing the certificate's slack), never a count per orbit; and the exact event, not the average, has the last word — r9's kill of averaged precession above τ₀ was System 1 keeping the last word. Test: belt scene, strain-triggered kicks vs the fixed 32/orbit, judged by the frozen B-gates at equal behaviour; count triggers per orbit.
4. **Slack is redundancy; size it by the tail, spend it by displacement.** — *"Redundancy is ambiguous because it seems like a waste if nothing unusual happens. Except that something unusual happens—usually."* (Antifragile, Nassim Nicholas Taleb.) Mechanism: buffers absorb variability at its point of entry; sizing by the average guarantees the tail gets through. Change: every slack budget (encounter skin, kick slack, allocator headroom) is sized by the measured p99 of the disturbance it absorbs, and **rebuilds are triggered by accumulated displacement (relative phase shear), not by element changes and not by the clock** — the probe killed the element-quantum currency outright (table below: even per-kick rebuilds miss 43 %, and with zero kicks 19/19). Test (ran): `python3 run_one.py B,1,16` and `python3 run_one.py nokick` (≈26 s); follow-up: displacement trigger at η ≈ the neighbour window on `crates/encounters`.
5. **Local efficiency and quotas are the enemies of system smoothness.** — *"[Teams] had the opportunity to 'stay efficient' by working in larger batches and passing work between departments. … When people are used to evaluating their productivity locally, they feel that a good day is one in which they did their job well all day."* (The Lean Startup, Eric Ries.) Mechanism: large batches maximize local utilization and create downstream spikes; single-piece flow at the constraint minimizes system latency. Change: the allocator (law.md: "one queue, ordered by slack") must **not** fill a frame to keep a core busy — small batches popped in slack order; and benchmark with an unbounded probe before setting any budget, because a quota anchors the result: the quota group produced 550 punch cards, the unquota'd 2,100 (The Skeptics' Guide). Test: 60-s bench with no frame budget, record the unconstrained p99 CPU-per-frame, then set budgets at measured-minus-headroom.
