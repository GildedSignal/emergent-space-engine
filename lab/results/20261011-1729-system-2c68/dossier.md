# Dossier — 20261011-1729-system-2c68 (lens: the whole system)

**Claim.** The plane re-derives, at every fired kick, the one thing its own representation says to carry: a tube is refit from scratch (`from_state`, 17.5 µs of a 35.6 µs kick, on 100% of kicks here), while carrying the same kicks exactly against the frozen tube holds a 10⁻⁶ relative error for a median of 34–790 kicks — so the re-anchored jet (law.md's adopted-but-unowned design) halves the plane's dominant cost (kick 36 → ~18.6 µs, 1.9×) with a bounded error type.

**Bottleneck.** Board focus #1: "fewer kicks per orbit at the same behaviour — kicks dominate the engine's cost." Six runs killed correctors and deferral (ceiling 1.1–1.2×); cadence attacks the kick *count*. This run measures the other lever on the same bottleneck: the kick's *cost*. Expected gain ≈ 2× on all kicked motion (the whole-universe item cost was 57.8 µs, of which ~18 is the rebind), and it deletes the per-kick compile-and-refit churn (a fresh `Entry` + catalogue slot + retired segment per kick). Nobody owns it: none of the 14 directed builds (`lab/queue.txt`) touches the rebind; the design sits in law.md ("Not adopted: a quantized (E, L) memo…; strong-field reuse is the jet…, **re-anchored**") with one gold-checker vote and no builder.

**The system view.** "Frozen generator + exactly carried increments + re-derive on a drift bound" already exists in the plane four times: the sigma-delta velocity carries (`step::quantize`), the binding's residual (`turn` + `offset`), the reader's ramp (`observe::credit`, the pending half-kick), the fractional-tick carries. The rebind and the ramp are secretly one mechanism — a correction on top of a pre-kick tube — at two window scales (one step vs one binding). The jet is the same mechanism with the window widened to the drift bound. This is principle 14 ("we only care about changes") applied to the one place the plane re-derives instead of carrying; it also dissolves the memo question the five earlier runs wrestled: the exact memo "never hits" because every kick's (E, L) is distinct — the jet does not need a hit, only that the frozen tube stays inside the bound.

**Predictions** (`predictions.md`, written before the probe): ≥80% of kicks fire → **100%, held**. Interval ≥50 at 10⁻⁶ on quiet bodies → held for two (790, 134), **missed for Hauler 1 (34)**. Orbiter shorter than Haulers by 3–30× → **inverted**: the Orbiter is 6–23× *longer*. Diagnosis: the interval is governed by the *variation* (surprise) of the perturbation, not its strength — the orbiter's tide+moon kicks are coherent (slowly rotating direction), while a hauler sees three planets at changing synodic geometries. That inversion is good news for scale: quiet orbits (the belt, the majority of body-time) are exactly where the jet runs longest. ≥1.7× speedup at R≥5 → held (1.9× everywhere).

**What would make it wrong.** (1) Deep flybys: a kick larger than the bound recompiles immediately — the jet degenerates to today's cost exactly where encounters already concentrate the cadence work; no loss, no gain. (2) Resonant locks pump coherently in one direction; drift becomes ~linear in kicks and the interval shortens (Hauler 1 may already be this shape). (3) The jet is **not bit-identical** to the shipped law: a kick's `quantize` would read the frozen tube + carries, so rounding realizations differ even though the error type and bound are the same — it is a law change in the same sense the re-anchored jet already is (law.md), and must re-face B5/B12/B13/B17, not assume the old digests. (4) My shadow's free-flight response is the *cheapest* carry; the real jet (derivative-scaled, per the memo) can only lengthen the intervals — these are lower bounds.

**Cost of adopting, fit with PRINCIPLES.** One change in `Work::kick`/`kick_clock` (carry the offset; recompile at the bound; the readout path already knows how to add corrections), plus re-facing the behaviour gates. Fits principle 1 ("the cheapest compute is compute not done"), 14 ("we only care about changes"), 13 (bounded error: the re-anchoring rule), and 3 ("cost follows change and entropy" — the measured interval is set by the perturbation's variation, not its size).

**The lens.** What it may have missed: I delivered one unification; other whole-system candidates (the two clocks merging, the light cone's three appearances) are already directed builds. The named hard cases above are where this elegant unification could break; the cheapest guard is a flyby case and one resonant case in the builder's first run.
