## Five lessons (each: quote → mechanism → change → cheapest test)

1. **Delete before you automate.** "Delete any part or process you can. You may have to add them back later. In fact, if you do not end up adding back at least 10% of them, then you didn't delete enough." / "Always wait until the end of designing a process—after you have questioned all the requirements and deleted unnecessary parts—before you introduce automation." — Elon Musk, Walter Isaacson. Mechanism: automation locks in the work it schedules; deleting first shrinks what must be scheduled. Change: no allocator/skin polish lands before kicks/orbit reaches its measured floor (0.3/orbit, H0190); anything deleted (correctors, higher-order splits — six runs) returns only behind a gate that fails without it. 2. **Judge survival, not drift.** "Survival comes first, truth, understanding, and science later." / "The average behavior of the market participant will not allow us to understand the general behavior of the market." — Taleb, Skin in the Game; cf. "Destruction is driven by single points of failure, which can happen in seconds" (Housel). Mechanism: an average error exponent cannot see an absorbing barrier; only the worst excursion can. 3. **Cap work in progress.** "Limited work in progress improves efficiency by preventing overwhelming overhead." — Cal Newport episode notes (Tim Ferriss Show; flagged: notes, not a book).
