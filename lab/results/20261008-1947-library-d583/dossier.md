## The five lessons (verbatim from the Readwise cache, `work/highlights.jsonl`)

1. **Certify absence, never presence.** "The Black Swan asymmetry allows you to be confident about what is wrong, not about what you believe is right." — Taleb, *The Black Swan*. Mechanism: a disproof is a proof (one evaluation, with expiry); a prediction's error compounds — measured above at 11%–77% of amplitude one event ahead. Change: the candidate tier's output must be *disproof* certificates (envelope-disjointness bounds with expiry), never predicted-encounter lists; the exact pass runs on what leaks through. Cheapest test: this probe (done); next — bound how many conjunctions ahead envelope disjointness *provably* holds per pair for one multiplier evaluation.
2. **Skewed book: near-zero premiums, rare payouts.** "The best description of my lifelong business in the market is 'skewed bets' … I try to make money infrequently, as infrequently as possible" — Taleb, *Fooled by Randomness*; "they make their money when the markets are panicking" — Lit Nomad. Mechanism: steady state should cost ~0 (premiums); real compute concentrates at the rare deep encounter — build the engine for the panic, not the catalog. Change: keep judging candidate tiers by work per *real* encounter at steady state (r8 v2's criterion), never by work per candidate pair. Cheapest test: the existing r8 belt harness, re-run with the CF tier deleted.
3. **Certify the billion, not the man.** "The reaction of one man could be forecast by no known mathematics; the reaction of a billion is something else again." — Asimov, *Foundation and Empire*. Mechanism: per-pair pay-once state is the unforecastable "man" (killed above); group envelopes — orbit families, phase-neighbour ranks (6 Oct gold: every real encounter within partner rank 4) — are the forecastable mass, O(N) not O(N²). Change: certificate state lives on groups; pairs exist only inside a group via phase rank. Cheapest test: adversarial formation belt (6 Oct gold's workload), measure amortized group-certificate work as N grows.
4. **Never sample below the event scale.** "Over a short time increment, one observes the variability of the portfolio, not the returns. In other words, one sees the variance, little else." — Taleb, *Fooled by Randomness*. Mechanism: polling faster than the surprise rate buys variance, not information — the lag-1 error above *is* that variance; a re-fit per frame would re-pay it (the plane's 99%-recompile pathology, 5 Oct). Change: a pair is touched only at its conjunction events or certificate expiry, never per frame/orbit. Cheapest test: instrument `behave --law plane` for distinct-kick vs re-timed checks applied to certificate expiry.
5. **Large batches feel efficient and break smoothness.** Teams "will ask to go back to the old way of working, in which they had the opportunity to 'stay efficient' by working in larger batches" — Ries, *The Lean Startup*. Mechanism: batch size trades felt efficiency against tail latency; per-orbit certificate sweeps are the "old way" and spike the p99 (B15). Change: renew certificates in increments sized by least-slack, not in per-orbit sweeps. Cheapest test: one cadence run — p99 frame cost with renewal batched per orbit vs slack-sized.

**Lens blind spot.** The trading/manufacturing analogies assume the expensive event is exogenous; here the "panic" (deep encounter) is produced by the same law that writes the certificates, so a certificate layer feeds back into encounter statistics — none of my quoted practitioners had to price that loop. And this lens sees books, not code: the probe is deliberately the smallest toy that could kill the premise; the belt's full law could differ in degree (not, I judge, in direction).
