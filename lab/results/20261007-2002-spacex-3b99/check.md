**Abstract.** Claim: a belt's live distinguishing information saturates at the belt's own element entropy — 39/51/63 bits/body at scar widths 2⁻¹⁶/⁻²⁰/⁻²⁴, independent of encounter rate — so per-body identity scars bust 32 bits/body at any width finer than ~2⁻¹³·⁶ and shipmem must split H_live by identity class (occupancy floor ≈ 23 bits/body). Reproduced bitwise: 4/5 hlive cases and the whole σ table (`measure_sig.py`, 67 s); flux 15,833,023 vs 15,833,626; 9.1 orbits (27.7 yr) to ceiling at N=10⁹, m=10⁻¹⁰; H_live 5.9×10⁻⁸ bits/body/game-s; reabsorption 0.010σ. Gain: kills per-body scars for anonymous belts (the 10⁹-in-8-GB decision). Not gold: the stored hot rows cannot be regenerated by the shipped `hlive.py`, and the headline time number silently assumes fixed per-body mass (a 0.1 M\* belt).

**Reasons.**

1. **Reproduction (mostly exact).** In a clean tree: the four non-hot cases of `hlive.py` (both seeds) are bitwise identical to `hlive_results.json` (44 s), and `measure_sig.py` regenerates `hlive_sig.json` bitwise (67 s). My independent 2-D crossing estimate of the conjunction flux (≈ 37/body/orbit) agrees in order with the author's 26.4. The saturated n=2000 histograms pin at log₂(2000) = 10.966 per field, exactly the sample bias the dossier discloses — the instrumentation note is honest and correct.
2. **Record defect (the gold blocker).** `hlive_results.json`'s hot case ran Poisson-thinned 50× (sim/expected = 31,668,064/1,583,362,697 = 1/50.0), but the shipped `hlive.py` has no thinning: one orbit of the shipped hot case simulates 5,272,854 conjunctions (measured, 2.0 s) where the stored run used ~105,560/orbit. `python3 hlive.py` as shipped is not "~70 s" and cannot regenerate the hot rows — the thinning lives only in `measure_sig.py`. File times show why: `run.log` (partial, cold cases only) at 20:18, `hlive.py` edited 20:23, results written 20:30 — the file was edited mid-run. Nothing load-bearing rests on hot (the ceiling, flux validation, N-scaling and reabsorption all come from cold/pert), and √50-scaled thinning preserves σ but not first-scar fractions, so the stored hot `frac_scarred` values are thinning artifacts regardless. This is precisely the "numbers the shipped code cannot regenerate" failure that has capped runs at silver before.
3. **Adversarial: the time-to-ceiling premise.** σ ∝ m√(N·t) at fixed per-body mass requires the 10⁹-body belt to total 0.1 M\* (m = 10⁻¹⁰) — a disk, not a dilute Keplerian belt; the linearized test-particle premise fails there. At fixed total belt mass, m ∝ 1/N and time-to-ceiling grows ∝ N, inverting the "~10 orbits" headline. What makes the design conclusion robust anyway — and the author measured it without leaning on it — is the embedded-perturber channel: λ_p = 0.147/body/orbit is N-independent, and one 10⁻⁷ M\* body saturates the belt's scars by t ≈ 30 at any N. The representation decision (no per-body scars for anonymous belts) survives every scaling; the headline *rate* number does not.
4. **Predictions.** `predictions.md` (20:06) predates the decisive results (20:30); order verified. Two of six failed and both are disclosed (scar rate 10⁴× off — the Fermi slip; the cold half of P6). A third failure is not disclosed: P4's "bits/body asymptotes below 32 in the cold case" is false at fine quanta (51 bits at 2⁻²⁰) — the saturation half of P4 held, the below-32 half flipped with P1.
5. **Novelty.** H0173 measured bits-per-collision for a ship gas (4 bits/collision; a rounding grid is required); components-98bc (gold) compressed the program side (0.126 bits/body). Neither measured the belt-entropy ceiling, the game-scale rate, or the scar-vs-occupancy split; the board carried exactly this as unmeasured ("decided by distinctions created per game-second"). New and on-goal (principle 24, "the memory floor is information-theoretic"; principle 6, store the generator).

**The one experiment to gold.** Make every stored number regenerable by the shipped code (move the √50 Poisson thinning into `hlive.py`'s hot case, or drop the hot rows) and re-anchor the time-to-ceiling on a stated belt-mass premise — fixed total mass, or the N-independent perturber channel — with the ceiling claim unchanged.
