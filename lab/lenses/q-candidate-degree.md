# Lens: candidate pairs must track encounters, not N (a directed question; the top scaling risk)

An outside review (GPT, 5 October) of our measurements: event processing, empty space and quiet orbits already scale optimally, but candidate discovery does not. At a fixed belt distribution, Binet-cone candidates per body grow with N (`crates/encounters`, rungs r8 v2 and v3: about 96 per body at 10³ bodies, 870 at 10⁴, 8,598 at 10⁵, so 430 million pairs at 10⁵): O(N²) pairs, although recurrence per pair, once set up, costs about 1.1 checks per real encounter. Lab run `20261003-1842-taleb-3ec1` adds that 99% of setup work lands on pairs that cannot meet within 100 orbits, and that one player burn re-sets up O(N) pairs.

**Question:** what certificate above the pair keeps candidate work proportional to the encounters that will actually happen within the horizon, not to the number of crossing orbits? The review's hierarchy: population envelope → orbital families → Binet pair → continued-fraction next hit. Consider envelopes of (U, V, W) points and a cone test between envelopes (the cone is a 2+1 Minkowski light cone, so set-to-set tests are causal-geometry tests), time-to-possible-encounter bounds for whole groups (kinetic hierarchies), and laziness (a pair is materialized only when its group's bound comes due). Every body stays exact and individual: no statistical stand-ins (Julien: "You cannot average a population"). Note the physics: at fixed distribution and fixed body size, real encounters per body also grow with N; the target is work proportional to real encounters within the horizon, plus a small constant per body.
- Build on copies of crates/encounters in your folder (never edit the crate). Measure candidate degree per body, setups per kick and misses (must be 0) against N from 10³ to 10⁶ at a fixed distribution, and against real encounters within the horizon. Held out: a densified belt, a fleet crossing a belt, a player burn.

Gold: candidate work per body bounded by C·log N (or by the real encounter rate) at a fixed distribution, with zero misses and a measured cost per kick.
