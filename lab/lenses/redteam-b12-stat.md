# Lens: red-team of B12's statistic (does it fail a law whose error never compounds?)

B12 (docs/law.md; `xtask/src/behave/handoff.rs`, `fit.rs`) judges "errors never compound" by the Jacobi constant's growth exponent on ships that cross a planet's sphere of influence at least 20 times, median over 16 frozen ships, limit 0.2, floor 1e-12. The Opus motion memo (`lab/refs/opus-motion/memo.md` §1, its script `lab/refs/opus-motion/synth.py`) claims two calibration faults: (1) errors that are bounded and depend only on the current state, planted on accurate trajectories, score 0.22–0.57 on these chaotic ships, so the gate would fail a law whose error never compounds; (2) accurate replicas lose 4–7 of 16 ships to chaos, a different set each time, so a lost ship says nothing about the law. The gate's positive control covers only a constant offset.

Attack premise first: is the growth exponent of a chaotic ship's Jacobi error a valid measure of compounding at all, and what would be? Then build, in your folder (never edit the crates or the gates; read them with `git show main:<path>`), the cheapest decisive probes: the planted bounded state-only error, a compounding error of the same initial size, and the reference itself at three tolerances, on B12's frozen ships. Report each one's statistic, and whether any threshold-preserving statistic separates bounded from compounding on chaotic ships (for example, the error's growth over an ensemble of one-quantum perturbations of the reference, or the error's distribution against the reference's own chaos). Return findings named by the invariant, each with a proposed disposition. No threshold may be loosened; a change must fail every planted compounding law that fails today.
