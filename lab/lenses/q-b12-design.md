# Lens: design B12's statistic from the requirement (a directed question; a red-team follows)

B12 (docs/law.md; `xtask/src/behave/handoff.rs`, `fit.rs`) must certify principle 13, "errors never compound", on ships that cross a planet's sphere of influence at least 20 times. Its growth exponent is broken (probe P2, `planted-bounded` in `xtask/src/behave/laws.rs`: a bounded state-only error fails at 0.49 while a compounding walk scores 0.31). A proposed recurrence ratio was rejected by `redteam-b12-stat` (`git show origin/claude/lab-20261006-0737-redteam-b12-stat-e5fe:lab/results/20261006-0737-redteam-b12-stat-e5fe/check.md`: a slow leak scores below a bounded error; echo fails B12 on Linux because the reference's convergence depends on the machine). Read both, the Opus motion memo §1 (`lab/refs/opus-motion/memo.md`), and the gate's code.

**Question:** what statistic, derived from the requirement and not from the scene, separates bounded from compounding error on chaotic ships, with a machine-independent ship set? State the game's horizon it certifies (how many periods), the smallest leak it can detect given the bounded error's amplitude (a leak below amplitude ÷ horizon is invisible in a short run), and how a chaos-sensitive ship's fate is judged (for example by the reference's fate under one-quantum perturbations).
- Build it in your folder on copies (never edit the gates or crates), and run it on: echo, `planted-bounded`, `scaled`, `planted-compound`, a leak at your stated detectable size, `planted-drift` and `planted-kick`, on Linux; report each statistic and its margin, and the reference's own result on two different floating-point paths (to show machine independence).
- Every threshold derived; none fitted to a law. Controls on both sides: echo, `planted-bounded` and `scaled` must pass; `planted-compound`, the leak, `planted-drift` and `planted-kick` must fail. Keep every process under 2 GB.

Gold: a statistic with derived thresholds that passes and fails all seven correctly with clear margins, machine-independent, with its horizon and detectable leak stated.
