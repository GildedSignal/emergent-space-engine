# Lens: hold each body's step in ticks (the price of a zero-byte calendar)

Read `lab/wiki/index.md`, `lab/wiki/calendar.md` and `lab/wiki/kick-cadence.md` first, and `docs/law.md` ("A step is a certificate"). Since the cadence merge (5aef8c9d), each body of the star's doll steps on its own clock through the motion law v2's time transform (`crates/universe/src/ext.rs`: a fictitious time s with a uniform Δs, so the real step Δt moves with the state). The calendar builder measured (`wt/calendar`, `examples/grid.rs`, 5.6×10⁵ game-s at 10⁴ bodies): every root ship's kick after its first changes its step (1,772 of 1,772, median 0.3%), carrying it 400-1,600 frames off any fixed grid, so a ship's calendar class cannot be derived from its id and the schedule costs 4.4 B a body instead of ~0 (at 10⁹: 4.4 GB of a ~6 GB budget).

The question: what does holding each body's real step in ticks cost, a power of two fixed per regime (it changes only when the body's certificate breaks: a secondary within its Hill-radius gap, a new regime), with the time transform kept only for the refinement near a secondary? Fly it on the shipped plane against the shipped law: the behaviour gates (`behave --law plane --gates B13,B17`), the energy register's drift (`J` bounded, the motion tests), the eccentricity walk a belt shows when it aliases the innermost secondary (`examples/cadence.rs`, `examples/centring.rs`), and the fraction of kicks whose step changes. Plant a control that must fail (a step too coarse for the frame's fall).

Gold: the verdict either way, with the gates' numbers both ways, the fraction of step changes per kick, and the schedule's bytes a body that would follow.
