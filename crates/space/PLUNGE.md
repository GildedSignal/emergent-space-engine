# Incoming arcs (v6 proposal; no new freeze)

A turning orbit's periapsis is not a coordinate for a radial fall. Anchor the
incoming arc at r0, retaining E, L and w0²=(dr/dτ)² there. In G=c=M=1, put
y=sqrt(r0/r-1), u0=1/r0 and u=(1+y²)u0. Then

    R(u) = w0² + y² u0 H(u)
    H(u) = 2m² - L²(u+u0) + 2L²(u²+u u0+u0²).

Here m²=1 for matter and 0 for light. At a rest anchor w0=0, the y in
|dr/dy|=2r0 y/(1+y²)² cancels the square root of y² exactly. Thus L=0 and
zero initial speed are ordinary inputs, not tiny substitute angular momenta.
The massive inverse proves R>0 over the complete inward interval using exact
Bernstein subdivision in Q(sqrt(X.X)); a barrier or unresolved interval refuses.
Light retains the exact dyadic b²<=27 capture decision of the existing inverse.

Four-point integer Gauss panels integrate angle, proper/affine time and clocks.
Geometric phase panels retain relative information over radius octaves. Split
quadratures select panels at compile time (2^-32 relative agreement); this is an
error estimator, not a proved continuum error bound. Compilation refuses beyond
8192 knots or 40 subdivisions; exact interval scratch is bounded separately.
Readout bisects a retained panel and evaluates its partial quadrature. It never
uses a previous readout. The endpoint is the exact requested radius, and later
readout refuses. Coefficients, anchor and endpoint belong to a plunge's identity;
the old Darwin memo key is absent, not invented.

The horizon is at r=2. Schwarzschild t is infinite there. Advanced time v=t+r*
is finite, since (E-sqrt(R))/f=(m²+L²/r²)/(E+sqrt(R)). Horizon arcs expose proper
or affine readout and finite advanced arrival; coordinate arrival is `None`.
No finite Schwarzschild horizon timestamp is claimed.

`Rebinding::ending_at` binds incoming surface arcs, retaining the exact event
remainder. The plane uses this at creation, kicks and handoffs, including rest.
It rounds a terminal deadline outward once to its integer clock; that endpoint
read returns the exact surface and the crash invalidates future motion. A new
binding cancels the old deadline. Its canonical kick-before-impact tie order
is unchanged. Bound/scattering paths outside the surface keep their old law.
Kick spacing uses local gravity/crossing time, never time left until the surface;
the endpoint is a deadline, so rebinding cannot create a shrinking-step tail.

Limits: outbound radial arcs needing an apoapsis are still refused. The plane's
clock and absorption ledger have not migrated to a horizon-regular chart;
horizon arcs are compiler support, not integrated black-hole qualification.
